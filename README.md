# LLM-Powered AD Research Wiki

A structured knowledge base for end-to-end autonomous driving research, built by ingesting academic papers with an LLM assistant (Claude). Each paper is read, discussed, and distilled into cross-linked wiki pages covering source summaries and reusable concept notes.

## Structure

```
raw/
  papers/      # Source papers (markdown, converted from arXiv HTML)
  assets/      # Figures extracted from papers
wiki/
  sources/     # One page per paper — summary, figures, tables, limitations
  concepts/    # Cross-paper concept notes (updated as new papers are ingested)
  index.md     # Master catalog of all wiki pages
  log.md       # Append-only ingest log
  research-directions.md  # Open research questions across the wiki, updated on every ingest and lint
CLAUDE.md      # Workflow instructions for the LLM assistant
```

- **Baseline rows are not commensurable by default.** There are *three* EPDMS lineages, not two: [Hydra-MDP++](wiki/sources/hydra-mdp-pp.md)'s own formula (C instead of HC, strict lane keeping, /22), whose rows ReCogDrive, SGDrive and WCog-VLA copy into v2 tables; the pre-fix NAVSIM-v2 evaluator; and the corrected one. **Of the 53 navtest v2 tables transcribed in the wiki, 37 mix rows on both sides of the residual-sign fingerprint** (recounted at the 2026-10-14 lint; the figure was "at least eleven") (SUV's is captioned "corrected" yet carries a pre-fix row; Metis pairs pre-fix anchors with a corrected-looking own row, per the [residual-sign heuristic](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md)), and the failure modes now include *non-headline configurations* cited unmarked (BrainWAM lists DynVLA’s SFT-only score against its published 91.7), *cross-version submetric contamination* (WCog-VLA’s v2 TransFuser row reuses its own v1 submetrics), *oracle Best-of-N scores in single-sample tables* (WCog-VLA lists AutoVLA at its 92.12 BoN rather than 89.11), and *rows with no attributable origin* ([CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) publishes NAVSIM-v2 scores for three methods whose own papers report none), and *ablation rows indistinguishable from external published rows* ([Drive-HWM](wiki/sources/drive-hwm.md): two of four Table IV values equal DriveVLA-W0's two published rows, one of them oracle Best-of-6). The newest mode is *a v1 score assembled from v2 sub-scores* ([PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md), the ninth mixing table and the eighth user of the shared block). [MomWorld](wiki/sources/momworld.md) is the ninth user of that block, and [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md)'s table is captioned "corrected" yet prints DiffusionDriveV2 at its pre-fix 85.5. The arithmetic checks also catch plain errors: [WALT](wiki/sources/walt.md)'s table prints a PRIX row with DAC 85.6 where 95.6 is the only value consistent with its score. Check submetrics and source configuration before comparing any two rows. **The shared v2 baseline block is now confirmed to be copied rather than recomputed** — Drive-HWM is its seventh user and prints the DriveSuprim row under the name "DriveSupervisor" while citing DriveSuprim's own arXiv entry. **Three papers now tabulate both columns instead of mixing them**, but two of them are the same lab — [WA-JEPA](wiki/sources/wa-jepa.md) and [CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) share ten of eleven authors and a GitHub org, and **they still place DriveVLA-W0's 86.1 in different columns.** The rule tightens to: trust a column label only for the paper's own row. *(lint 2026-10-06: the mixing-table count needs redoing. Two independent tables print DiffusionDrive at a corrected-like 88.2, so the circulating 84.5 now reads as pre-fix; up to three tables counted as mixing on the strength of that row may drop out, and ReDrive's "corrected" table may join. See [Research Directions](wiki/research-directions.md).)*
- **The representation is worth several PDMS — but mostly when the planner is weak.** [Unified Driving Tokens](wiki/sources/unified-driving-tokens.md) moves NAVSIM 85.5 → 91.8 by changing only a tokenizer's objectives under a fixed 20M readout; [DriveLaW](wiki/sources/drivelaw.md) spans 84.1 → 89.1 across representation families under a fixed planner. [CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) supplies the wiki's **first complete representation × planner 2×2** and finds they are largely **substitutes** (+5.0 / +5.2 alone, +1.1 / +1.3 together; interaction −3.9). Both prior numbers were measured in the cell where the other factor is weak. See [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
- **Two rules now govern world-model supervision, and they are orthogonal.** *Objective form* follows target entropy — deterministic regression fails in proportion to it ([WA-JEPA](wiki/sources/wa-jepa.md), [DriveFuture](wiki/sources/drivefuture.md)). *Target content* follows the horizon — [Drive-HWM](wiki/sources/drive-hwm.md) ablates the prediction target separately for its 8-step and 1-step branches in one codebase and the ordering fully inverts: **optical flow is best far out, RGB is best one step ahead.** Nobody has crossed the two rules. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **Generation quality and planning quality are decoupled.** [ReWorld](wiki/sources/reworld.md) improves nuScenes FVD 81.3 → 61.9 and NAVSIM PDMS 89.1 → 90.4 in one paper, via *disjoint* mechanisms — and never measures whether the video-side one helps planning. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md) *([SimVTA](wiki/sources/simvta.md), 2026-10-14: one intervention, a video loss on 3DGS renders of the policy's own trajectories during RL, moves FVD 105.4 → 46.3 and PDMS 89.1 → 91.3 together, but without the initialization's video numbers the two are still not separated.)*
- **Almost everything above 92.0 PDMS scores its own candidates.** Of the highest non-BoN NAVSIM-v1 entries, all but one train a scorer or an RL reward against the benchmark's own metric. The exception is [Drive-HWM](wiki/sources/drive-hwm.md) (no scorer, no RL), whose own paper cannot decide between 93.8 and 93.3. The recipe traces back to [Hydra-MDP++](wiki/sources/hydra-mdp-pp.md): offline simulation of a fixed vocabulary with ground-truth perception, one distilled head per sub-score, and a grid-searched weighted cost that is its single largest ablation effect (+1.5 PDMS). The two newest entries above 92 fit the pattern in different ways: [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) trains a scorer on simulator sub-scores, and [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) trains its generator on pairs labelled by the evaluator's own collision and drivable-area gates. [World4Scorer](wiki/sources/world4scorer.md), at 94.0 the newest top entry, fits it directly: it keeps DrivoR's generator and replaces the scorer with one whose candidate states are supervised by simulator outcomes. See [Selection-Based Planning](wiki/concepts/selection-based-planning.md)
- **Training-seed variance on NAVSIM has been measured three times, and it is small; in closed loop it is not.** *(2026-10-14: four times; [CLOVER](wiki/sources/clover.md)'s three seeds span 0.014 PDMS and EPDMS.)* [ForeDrive](wiki/sources/foredrive.md) retrains a 118M single-camera planner from three seeds: **89.7–89.9 PDMS, sd 0.1**. [SimVTA](wiki/sources/simvta.md) repeats four RL post-training configurations of a 2.5B world-action model from one checkpoint: **sd 0.06–0.1**, with the same ordering on every seed. [ManeuverFlow](wiki/sources/maneuverflow.md): **sd 0.04–0.06** on navtest for a ResNet-34 pipeline, and **0.64–0.92** for the same three checkpoints on the 288-scenario WorldEngine closed loop. Every earlier figure here was *sampler*-seed noise with fixed weights ([WA-JEPA](wiki/sources/wa-jepa.md) 0.053, [CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) 0.013, [PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md) 0.24–0.30). One recipe and three seeds; large pretrained models remain unmeasured. See [Evaluation Variance](wiki/concepts/evaluation-variance.md)
- **A predicted future can help a planner without being a good forecast.** ForeDrive's planner gains +0.4–0.6 from consuming predicted latents, but ignores their order, gains only +0.2 from the real future, and plans *worst* with the most accurate forecaster (latent cosine 0.843 → 87.9 PDMS vs 0.759 → 89.9). A wrong future costs more (−2.4) than none (−1.4). See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **NAVSIM v1 and v2 reward different plans, and a comfort check taken from the metric moves v2 by 1.6.** [World4Scorer](wiki/sources/world4scorer.md) scores frozen candidate pools with simulator sub-scores: the two ladders choose different candidates in 43% of scenes, 98% of the gap is extended comfort, and choosing for v2 costs 1.3 PDMS on v1. Re-ranking with the benchmark's EC criterion against the previous plan lifts EC 77.0 → 92.8 and EPDMS +1.6, on its own planner and on DrivoR alike. Its scorer, swapped onto DrivoR's fixed candidates, is worth +0.50 PDMS against an oracle of 99.33. See [Selection-Based Planning](wiki/concepts/selection-based-planning.md)
- **A published planning number is a pipeline number.** [DriveFuture](wiki/sources/drivefuture.md) is the first paper here to report the same checkpoint with and without a scorer: 30.9 → 34.6 (its mechanism) → **55.5** (GTRS-Dense over 100 proposals). Most papers report the pipeline while describing the generator
- **The first closed-loop reproduction of NAVSIM leaders inverts the ranking.** [Qwen-Drive-1.0](wiki/sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md) re-runs [SimWAM](wiki/sources/simwam.md) (91.5 PDMS, the wiki's best video-prior world-action model) and [DriveWAM](wiki/sources/drivewam.md) in **AlpaSim**, a reactive simulator over 916 reconstructed real-log scenarios: SimWAM is **last** on at-fault score (0.30) and Alpamayo-R1, which reports no NAVSIM number at all, is **first** (0.58). DriveWAM's flattering 0.53 comes from **35% progress** — it barely moves, and is struck from behind in 56% of scenarios. See [AlpaSim](wiki/concepts/alpasim-benchmark.md)
- **Driving adaptation barely reaches the planner.** The same paper trains a matched planner on three backbones — unadapted Qwen3.5-4B, +driving VQA, +3D perception — and the **entire adaptation is worth +0.08 RFS** (7.88 → 7.91 → 7.96), against +0.50 for RL on the action head alone, while the same supervision moves driving QA +6.55 and causal reasoning +38.39. **Driving knowledge and driving competence are separate acquisitions.** Sixth consecutive paper where the named mechanism is the smaller term
- **A driving VLM can keep its general capability, and almost none do.** Across 15 general benchmarks and 13 models under one protocol, Qwen-Drive scores **66.41 against its base model's 67.40** while Alpamayo-1.5 scores **14.65**, MiMo-Embodied **26.53** and UniDriveVLA **48.73** — and the *unadapted* base model beats every driving-specialized model on driving QA. The distinguishing variable looks like the **data mixture** (26% general-purpose), not the architecture. See [General-Capability Retention](wiki/concepts/general-capability-retention.md)
- **Vision-language pretraining does not contain driving 3D structure.** A converged BEV head on **frozen** Qwen-initialized features trails a dedicated detector on the *same* features by 6.34 mAP; unfreezing recovers +10.46. Third backbone family to price off-the-shelf use, after a video DiT (−6.42 PDMS) and DINOv2 geometric distillation (LoRA collapses). See [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
- **Three papers, one backbone, four attention masks, and every effect lives on navhard.** [SimWAM](wiki/sources/simwam.md), [Metis](wiki/sources/metis.md) and [SUV](wiki/sources/suv.md) share Wan2.2-5B, a ~1B action expert and nearly the same recipe, and differ mainly in who may attend to whom. On navtest every non-bidirectional mask lands within 0.5 EPDMS. On navhard:
  - Letting the future read the action is worth **+2.2** (Metis).
  - Letting the action read the imagined future is worth **+4.1** (SUV), against +0.3 on navtest.
  - The bidirectional mask is **worst** (Metis), which conflicts with SUV unless the damage is the feedback loop.
  - Outside that family, [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) finds bidirectional the best cell on navtest, but only with modality-specific branches (+0.4; −0.1 in a single DiT). The mask interacts with the block design.

  See [Attention Masks in World-Action Models](wiki/concepts/wam-attention-masks.md)
- **Grounded reasoning helps, but only after RL.** [GRAVA](wiki/sources/grava.md) gives the wiki's most graded CoT ablation. With every variant RL-trained, closed-loop score on its internal long-tail benchmark goes: action-only 74.8 → coarse reasoning 77.9 → box-grounded objects 85.5 → full grounded reasoning 90.1. Without RL, full reasoning scores 73.1, below action-only *with* RL. Editing the trace edits the action (0.44 → 0.82 normalized PDMS), but the trace's final maneuver is copied from the expert trajectory at annotation time. See [Chain-of-Thought for AD](wiki/concepts/chain-of-thought-for-ad.md)
- **The sign of a row's submetric residual appears to reveal its EPDMS protocol.** Recomputing EPDMS from a row's own submetrics and subtracting the reported value gives **+1 to +4 for pre-fix rows and slightly negative for corrected rows**. It flips exactly on the two rows known in both conventions (Human Agent +4.1 / −0.1; DriveFine +1.2 / −0.8). Three exceptions remain (a fourth, DiffusionDrive 84.5, now reads as a pre-fix row labelled corrected), so it is a heuristic. See [Residual sign](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md)
- **A world model that is shown the future still loses to a policy that is not.** [AD-E2E-JEPA](wiki/sources/ad-e2e-jepa.md) trains no policy: it rolls a JEPA world model out over 256–8,192 vocabulary trajectories and keeps the one closest to the *ground-truth future frame*. That oracle-goal search recovers **67.3–72.9 EPDMS** on the full navtest, against **85.4** for the same paper's goal-free imitation model and about 94.5 for the human trajectory it is aiming at. The scores are a world-model diagnostic and stay off the leaderboard. The paper also shows a 100-scene subset ordering its own four variants differently from the full 12,146. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md) and [Evaluation Variance](wiki/concepts/evaluation-variance.md)
- **A geometric loss can be worth the same on navtest and navhard, and a "v1" score can be built from v2 sub-scores.** [PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md) co-denoises video, metric depth and ego motion in a 15.2B Cosmos 3 model and ties depth to motion through a LiDAR point loss: **+1.9 EPDMS on navtest, +1.9 on navhard, +2.1 HD-Score on HUGSIM**, the first mechanism here that is not several times larger on navhard. Generating depth alone is a null. The paper concedes the coupling itself is not isolated. Its printed **"PDMS 91.4" is kept off the v1 ladder**: all four of its rows carry identical sub-scores under both protocols and a PDMS equal to their closed form, which no other method in its table does. It also measures a **recorded-versus-recorded FVD floor of 91.5** at 600 clips, which makes the wiki's unqualified NAVSIM FVD numbers unrankable, and a **sampler sd of 0.24–0.30**, 5–20× earlier figures. 9.4 GPU-seconds per plan. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md) and [NAVSIM Benchmark](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md)
- **An encoder and a planner are enough for 90.8 EPDMS, and the world model that shaped them is worth +0.7.** [ReDrive](wiki/sources/redrive.md) trains a V-JEPA 2 encoder and a 103M flow-matching planner with a trajectory-conditioned JEPA future predictor that is discarded at inference. Its own ablation: V-JEPA 2 over DINOv2 **+5.5**, unfreezing the encoder **+3.5**, future prediction **+0.7**, planner adaptation through the frozen predictor **+0.3**. It is 0.9 EPDMS behind WA-JEPA, which generates futures at inference with the same encoder family, and it is the best no-access model on navhard (34.4). Two cross-paper consequences: **L1 regression on future latents helps here where WA-JEPA found it harmful** (so the entropy rule needs "unless action-conditioned or never consumed"), and **fine-tuning an image encoder under a predictive loss does not close the gap to V-JEPA 2** (DINOv2 84.4 vs 89.9). Its v2 table is WA-JEPA's corrected column with WA-JEPA removed. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md) and [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
- **An ablation table can be too clean, and a leaderboard can be missing its authors' own paper.** [MomWorld](wiki/sources/momworld.md) adds a momentum variable to a latent world model and feeds a shared future memory to a GTRS-Dense trajectory scorer: 90.2 PDMS, 90.1 EPDMS, **42.8 navhard, which is +1.1 over its base** with Stage-2 progress up 12 points and NC / TTC down 5–6. Its navhard table reuses [DriveFuture](wiki/sources/drivefuture.md)'s baseline rows and omits all six entries above 42.8, including DriveFuture (55.5), with which it shares three authors. Its three ablation tables contain columns that are exact functions of each other (L2@6 − TPC@6 constant to sd 0.008 over 27 rows; NAVSIM PDMS linear in nuScenes L2 across two different base models), so the wiki records the page at **low confidence** and cites no component effect from it. The "long-horizon" gain over MomAD is 34–39% at 1–2 s and 6% at 6 s. See [Evaluation Variance](wiki/concepts/evaluation-variance.md) and [Navhard](wiki/concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md)
- **On a strong frozen world model, the planner's output space is a small lever.** [WALT](wiki/sources/walt.md) freezes EponaV2 and changes only what its trajectory head generates. Raw waypoints, a learned trajectory latent, JEPA-style trajectory pretraining and REPA-style alignment to a trajectory encoder all land within 0.07 PDMS (89.42–89.49). Aligning the latent to the frozen world model's features gives **+0.35** (89.83 PDMS; 87.9 EPDMS, +0.6, mostly extended comfort). That matches [ReWorld](wiki/sources/reworld.md)'s +0.4 for a different alignment on a different backbone and [ReDrive](wiki/sources/redrive.md)'s +0.3 for planner adaptation through a frozen predictor: **three post-hoc ways of coupling a planner to a fixed predictive model, each worth a few tenths.** RL on the same EponaV2 base is worth about +1.0 PDMS / +1.6 EPDMS. See [Action Tokenization](wiki/concepts/action-tokenization.md) and [Diffusion Planner](wiki/concepts/diffusion-planner.md)
- **A safety rule is worth more as a training signal than as a test-time check, and a computed verdict is not worse than a learned one.** [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) re-implements NAVSIM's collision and drivable-area gates as a zero-parameter rule and uses it twice on a one-camera Cosmos 3 video world-action model. On ground-truth maps it builds 462 winner/loser pairs for a preference loss: **+0.92 EPDMS** (95% CI +0.71 to +1.13), to 91.96 PDMS / 91.61 EPDMS in a single pass. On maps predicted from the camera it gates a second sample: **+0.30** on the base policy and **nothing** after the distillation. At two candidates no learned verifier is detectably better than the rule, including one given the same predicted map (−0.01, CI ±0.15), and gating matters more than the verdict source. The rule is itself lossy (63% recall on drivable-area violations even with ground-truth maps). See [Inference-Time Safety](wiki/concepts/inference-time-safety.md) and [Selection-Based Planning](wiki/concepts/selection-based-planning.md)
- **Sixty-four futures, and the future is still the small term.** [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) generates 64 trajectory–future pairs jointly in 233 ms and scores each trajectory against its own future: 93.4 PDMS on navtrain, 94.0 on trainval, 91.5 corrected EPDMS. Its ablation prices each part: one trajectory 84.1; 32 trajectories with a simulator-trained, history-only scorer **92.3 (+8.2)**; generating a paired future **+0.6**; letting the scorer read it **+0.4**. That is the second per-candidate-future result after [DA-WAM](wiki/sources/da-wam.md) (+0.15), same sign and same size class. On v2 its ego progress (92.2) is 4.8 above the human agent's while driving direction, lane keeping and history comfort are the lowest in its table, because its scorer learned the v1 components. See [Selection-Based Planning](wiki/concepts/selection-based-planning.md) and [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **A world model can be the training environment instead of the policy, and the result ships.** [Learning to Drive from a World Model](wiki/sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md) trains a small openpilot steering policy on its own rollouts inside a future-anchored DiT world model (or a reprojective simulator), labelled at every step by a plan head that sees where the human went; no reward. Closed-loop lane-centre tests go from **5/24** (behaviour cloning, which has the *best* held-out error, 0.361) to **24/24** (0.394). Both on-policy policies were deployed to real cars. The two simulators are not separated by the tests, the field cohorts are unmatched, and nothing is ablated. See [Data-Driven Driving Simulators](wiki/concepts/data-driven-simulators.md)
- **A world model with no world-model loss, and a future target that hurts.** [ResWorld](wiki/sources/resworld.md) feeds its world model only frame-to-frame differences of ego-aligned BEV scene queries and trains it through the planner alone. Supervising its predicted map toward the real next frame raises nuScenes collision 0.17% → 0.21%, and the baseline-architecture first-pass trajectory keeps most of the gain (0.28 → 0.18% against 0.17% for the full model). Second paper after [DriveFuture](wiki/sources/drivefuture.md) whose future latent has no prediction target, and a third vote, with WA-JEPA, that regression toward the future can be worse than nothing. Effects are a few nuScenes samples, single runs. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **Training data alone moves navhard several points, and a planner that sees no images still fails Stage 2.** [SimScale](wiki/sources/simscale.md) co-trains three planner families on 3DGS-rendered scenes in which the ego has been pushed off the log and a pseudo-expert labels the recovery: **+5.1 to +8.6 navhard EPDMS** against +0.8 to +2.9 on navtest, with no change to the model. Two unscored planners leave the Stage-2 lane-keeping band (47.9 → 58.3, 46.4 → 60.8), which the wiki had credited to candidate scoring. And PDM-Closed, planning on ground-truth state, loses **25.6 points of Stage-2 lane keeping** without any rendering, so much of navhard's Stage-2 drop is in its scenarios. Whether the training gain is recovery or familiarity with 3DGS images is not separated, and the paper's tables run 0.5–1.9 points above its own scaling figure. See [Navhard and OOD Evaluation](wiki/concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md) and [Data-Driven Simulators](wiki/concepts/data-driven-simulators.md)
- **Bench2Drive's leaders are approaching the expert they imitate.** [Think2Drive](wiki/sources/think2drive.md), a privileged BEV planner trained by model-based RL inside a DreamerV3 world model of CARLA, generated Bench2Drive's official training data and scores **91.85 DS** there (PDM-Lite, the rule-based alternative expert, 97.02). The best Think2Drive-protocol student in the wiki is 5.6 below it and LinkVLA (protocol undisclosed) 0.84 below. In its own paper the world-model learner beats PPO with identical engineering, 83.8 vs 57.5 DS, and its camera student keeps only 36.4. See [Bench2Drive](wiki/concepts/bench2drive.md)
- **Reinforcement learning from cameras exists on Bench2Drive, and it trails imitation.** [Raw2Drive](wiki/sources/raw2drive.md) trains a camera encoder into Think2Drive's privileged world-model latent space, keeps its dynamics and reward heads, and fine-tunes its policy by RL: **71.36 DS**, +7 over the imitation baselines it compares against and about 20 below the best imitation methods here, and 20.5 below the teacher it is built from. A reward head learned on camera latents breaks its training (83.5 → 46.6 on a 10-clip subset). See [RL for AD](wiki/concepts/rl-for-ad.md)
- **RL in 3DGS works through its hand-written parts.** [RAD](wiki/sources/rad.md) post-trains a camera policy with PPO in 3,968 reconstructions of real driving and cuts its collision ratio from 0.229 (imitation-only) to 0.089 on 337 held-out clips. But PPO without four rule-directed auxiliary losses is no better than imitation (0.249), other cars are log-replayed, and a second 3DGS benchmark from the same institutions ([Senna-2](wiki/sources/senna2.md)'s) scores the same policy at 0.281 and halves its advantage over VADv2. See [RL for AD](wiki/concepts/rl-for-ad.md)
- **A simulator can supply a world model's targets instead of the policy's inputs, and a wrong target is worse than none.** [SimVTA](wiki/sources/simvta.md) post-trains Epona with Flow-GRPO on the PDM reward and keeps its video loss running, with targets rendered by a 3DGS simulator along each explored trajectory. RL alone takes 86.2 → 89.1 PDMS; the rendered-target video loss adds +2.2 (91.3, three seeds), but only when its gradients reach the shared encoder; renders cached once from the initial policy give +1.3; **shuffling the targets among a scene's trajectories gives 83.1, 6.0 below no video**. The control that would show off-log targets matter, keeping the logged-video loss during RL, is not run. See [Data-Driven Driving Simulators](wiki/concepts/data-driven-simulators.md) and [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **More candidates add oracle headroom the selector does not collect.** [ManeuverFlow](wiki/sources/maneuverflow.md), a ResNet-34 generate–score–refine planner (90.58 PDMS / 90.14 EPDMS), freezes its one-step generator and finds best-of-N rising from 92.2 at 8 candidates to 94.3 at 64 and 94.85 at 256, past the human agent, while its deployed plan moves from 90.06 to 90.58 and stops. Its scorer, trained on the evaluator's own labels, picks the pool's best candidate first in 40% of scenes. The same paper finds one shared future forecast, sampled along each candidate's path, worth +1.58 PDMS, where DA-WAM found a shared future harmful (−0.50): the difference may be the readout. See [Best-of-N](wiki/concepts/best-of-n.md) and [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **The geometry backbone under GeoWAM is a planner in its own right, and its head samples.** [DVGT-2](wiki/sources/dvgt-2.md) reconstructs metric point maps, relative ego pose and the trajectory in one 1.8B streaming transformer (88.6 PDMS without a NAVSIM stage, 90.3 / 89.6 EPDMS as DVGT-2-NAVSIM, the rows other papers call "DVGT-2"). It never ablates its geometry objective, its v2 margin crosses evaluators (+0.7 on one scale), and its truncated-diffusion head means GeoWAM's +0.6 / +4.9 over it prices a head swap together with forecasting.
- **Training the generator against its own scorer improves ranking, not candidates.** [CLOVER](wiki/sources/clover.md) retrains DrivoR's stack against the NAVSIM evaluator and reaches 94.5 PDMS; by its own oracle/regret identity +0.69 of its +0.74 is better ranking and +0.05 better candidates. It gains nothing on navhard (48.3 = DrivoR), and on NAVSIM-v2 its diverse pool scores extended comfort 35.0 until a second, single-mode planner anchors the choice (90.4, below the 90.66 a later paper measures for DrivoR).
- **A fixed vocabulary made dense enough competes, and the earliest copy of a table matters.** [SparseDriveV2](wiki/sources/sparsedrivev2.md) factorizes trajectories into 1,024 paths × 256 speed profiles and scores the combinations in two passes: 92.0 PDMS and 90.1 corrected EPDMS on a ResNet-34, and 89.15 DS on Bench2Drive with no metric supervision at all. It reports no latency and no oracle, so whether density buys candidates or easier ranking is open. Its March 2026 v2 table is the earliest print of four rows that later tables carry, which withdraws the wiki's reading that two groups independently confirmed the DiffusionDriveV2 and SparseDriveV2 evaluator-fix deltas.
- **"Latent world model RL" can mean RL that never touches the world model.** [WorldRFT](wiki/sources/worldrft.md) samples trajectories from its planning head and rewards −1 for each point that overlaps an annotated agent box. On nuScenes that is the collision metric itself (0.15% → 0.05%, a handful of samples), and on NAVSIM the same reward moves drivable area and progress (+0.8 each) more than collisions (+0.3). Its ablations price a frozen VGGT (+1.3 PDMS), the planning head and RL, but never the world-model loss in its title. The wiki had listed its reward as the PDM score, and the NAVSIM-v2 row six papers print for it is not in its paper.
- **The latent world-model line's own root measured what its descendants stopped measuring.** [LAW](wiki/sources/law.md) (2024) runs the action-free control: next-latent prediction without the action buys about a third of its nuScenes gain, and conditioning on the planner's own trajectory the rest. That input is not detached, so part of the gain may be a second imitation loss. The loss is worth +7.1 PDMS on a weak 77.5 base, +2.2 DS in CARLA inside the run spread, and turns harmful at a 10 s horizon. A second "LAW" NAVSIM row (83.8) in two later tables is in neither version of the paper.
- Every leaderboard claim in this wiki carries a **comparison-scope caveat** — most papers omit the actual frontier from their tables. See [NAVSIM Benchmark](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md).

## Open Threads

Questions the wiki has surfaced but not resolved, in rough order of how much they'd change the picture. The full list, grouped by theme and updated on every ingest and lint, is in [Research Directions](wiki/research-directions.md).

1. **Does test-time future imagination help at all? It now depends on the benchmark.** On navtest, three results say no. [SimWAM](wiki/sources/simwam.md)'s mask ablation loses nothing without future access (90.3 vs. 90.2); [DriveLaW](wiki/sources/drivelaw.md) finds early denoising latents beat near-clean futures; [SUV](wiki/sources/suv.md) finds access worth only +0.3. **On navhard, SUV measures +4.1 for the same access**, and [Metis](wiki/sources/metis.md) finds the bidirectional variant worst. The deciding experiment is SimWAM's isolated checkpoint scored on navhard, plus the missing one-way/one-way cell of the mask family. **The one configuration that is positive on navtest is a future per candidate, read by a scorer**: +0.15 in [DA-WAM](wiki/sources/da-wam.md) and +0.4 in [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md), both small and both single runs. *(lint 2026-10-06: not the only positive configuration. A shared geometric future is worth +1.7 PDMS to [GeoWorldAD](wiki/sources/geoworldad.md), not compute-matched, and a shared DINOv3-latent future +0.4 to +0.6 to [ForeDrive](wiki/sources/foredrive.md), whose planner ignores the forecast's order and plans best with its least accurate forecaster.)* See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
2. **Is NAVSIM-v1 saturated?** Best-of-6 reaches 94.8 = the human ground-truth score (Curious-VLA). If oracle selection already matches the logged human, single-sample gains above ~93 may be measuring selection quality rather than driving quality. *(lint 2026-10-06: [World4Scorer](wiki/sources/world4scorer.md) reaches 94.0 single-pass on navtrain; on a fixed 64-candidate pool the oracle is 99.33 against 94.19 for its scorer, so about 5 points of selection headroom remain.)* See [Best-of-N Sampling](wiki/concepts/best-of-n.md). *(lint 2026-10-14: partly answered for candidates. [ManeuverFlow](wiki/sources/maneuverflow.md)'s frozen one-step ResNet-34 generator passes the human score at best-of-256 (94.85) while its own selector delivers 90.6; the open part is selection, see [Best-of-N](wiki/concepts/best-of-n.md#maneuverflow-oracle).)* *(2026-10-14: [CLOVER](wiki/sources/clover.md) reaches 94.5 single-pass, training split unstated; its 64-candidate oracle is 99.4–99.8, and by its own decomposition its gain over DrivoR is almost all ranking.)*
3. **Are video-prior gains about scale or objective?** SimWAM shows video-prior scale barely matters on navtest (Wan2.1-1.3B 90.2 ≈ Wan2.2-5B 90.3), and DriveWAM shows dropping the video objective is catastrophic. **But [Metis](wiki/sources/metis.md) finds the same 1.3B-vs-5B swap roughly equal on navtest and −2.4 on navhard.** Scale may matter exactly where navtest cannot see it. See [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
4. **Do PDMS gains transfer to closed-loop? The first multi-method answer is no.** Most 90+ PDMS methods still report no Bench2Drive, HUGSIM or navhard result, and stage-2 navhard remains near 40 EPDMS for everything measured *(lint 2026-10-06: for unscored planners trained on real data; scored entries above 50 combined sit higher, and SimScale's simulation data lifts unscored planners' Stage-2 lane keeping by 10–14 points)*. [Qwen-Drive-1.0](wiki/sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md) now supplies the first head-to-head check: in **AlpaSim**, a reactive simulator over 916 reconstructed real-log scenarios, the at-fault ranking is **Alpamayo-R1 0.58 > DriveWAM 0.53 > Alpamayo-1.5 0.45 > Qwen-Drive-RL 0.37 > SimWAM 0.30**, while the NAVSIM ranking of the same methods runs **SimWAM 91.5 > Qwen-Drive 90.7 > DriveWAM 90.1** with both Alpamayo variants reporting no NAVSIM number at all. The two best closed-loop methods are not on the leaderboard and the best leaderboard method is last. Neither WAM behaves well for the reason its paper predicts: DriveWAM is degenerately cautious (35% progress, 56% all-event close encounters) and SimWAM degenerately aggressive (22% at-fault, 19% off-road). These are third-party reproductions at one run each, and the AlpaSim score is a ratio that flatters near-stationary policies — but a 1.4-point PDMS lead carried no closed-loop information at all. The same paper also finds that **RL shaped against PDMS moves closed-loop behaviour in the opposite direction from how it moves PDMS**: NAVSIM ego progress 82.4 → 84.8, AlpaSim progress 54% → 48%, with off-road halved. See [AlpaSim](wiki/concepts/alpasim-benchmark.md). *(lint 2026-10-14: two more closed-loop data points, both weak. On HUGSIM's 345-scenario set [SimVTA](wiki/sources/simvta.md) (91.3 PDMS) scores 38.8 HD-Score against DrivoR's 35.7 (93.1 PDMS), a second lower-PDMS world-model planner above DrivoR after WA-JEPA; neither has a matched control. On the new [WorldEngine benchmark](wiki/concepts/worldengine-benchmark.md), 288 3DGS scenes from navtest logs, three of four methods keep their NAVSIM order and VADv2 drops to last, and reactive IDM traffic moves every method by only 0.5–2.8 points.)*
5. **Is navtest measuring the wrong thing for world models? The evidence is now systematic.** Four independent navtest-small / navhard-large effects: [GeoWAM](wiki/sources/geowam.md) future geometry +0.6 / +4.9 *(2026-10-14: together with a head swap; [DVGT-2](wiki/sources/dvgt-2.md)'s head is truncated diffusion)*; SUV future access +0.3 / +4.1; Metis asymmetric mask +0.5 / +2.2; Metis video-prior scale ≈0 / +2.4. Caveat: the navhard split size is itself disputed (244 vs. 450 Stage-1 scenes; two papers now say 450). **First exception**: [PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md)'s depth–motion loss is worth +1.9 on navtest and +1.9 on navhard, so the asymmetry belongs to inference-path mechanisms (access, masks, prior scale), not to every world-model addition *(lint 2026-10-06: nor to world models as such: [SimScale](wiki/sources/simscale.md)'s 3DGS training data gives the same shape, +0.8 to +2.9 navtest against +5.1 to +8.6 navhard, with an unchanged model)*. Originally: GeoWAM adds future-geometry forecasting to DVGT-2 and gains **+0.6 EPDMS on navtest but +4.9 on navhard** — the same change worth eight times more under the reactive protocol, exactly as a world-model thesis predicts. Every world-model paper in the wiki optimizes navtest; only two report navhard at all *(lint 2026-10-06: now at least seven: GeoWAM, SUV, Metis, PhysWAM, ReDrive, MomWorld and DriveFuture)*. If the asymmetry replicates, the field has been evaluating anticipation on a benchmark where anticipation barely pays.
6. **Is reasoning a rationale or a verbalized action?** [GRAVA](wiki/sources/grava.md) shows the action follows the trace, but its trace ends in a maneuver copied from the expert trajectory. No paper yet builds the final decision only from grounded object-level evidence and tests whether the ladder survives. See [Chain-of-Thought for AD](wiki/concepts/chain-of-thought-for-ad.md)
7. **Comparison-scope inflation is systemic.** Nearly every ingested paper claims SOTA against a table that omits the actual frontier. The wiki tracks this per-paper, but it makes cross-paper ranking unreliable in principle.
8. **What is a detectable effect on navtest?** [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) supplies the first paired, per-scene confidence intervals: about ±0.2 EPDMS for the difference between two checkpoints, before any training-seed variance, which has now been measured for three recipes (sd 0.04–0.1 PDMS: [ForeDrive](wiki/sources/foredrive.md), [SimVTA](wiki/sources/simvta.md), [ManeuverFlow](wiki/sources/maneuverflow.md); *2026-10-14: a fourth, [CLOVER](wiki/sources/clover.md), range 0.014*) and once in closed loop, where it is ten times larger. Many of the mechanism effects recorded here are 0.3–0.6 from single runs ([WALT](wiki/sources/walt.md) +0.4, [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md)'s pairing +0.6 and future-aware scoring +0.4, ReDrive's future prediction +0.7). And an ablation table can fail in the other direction: [MomWorld](wiki/sources/momworld.md)'s columns are exact functions of each other. See [Evaluation Variance](wiki/concepts/evaluation-variance.md)
9. **Should a safety rule be checked at inference or trained in?** DriveReferee finds the same geometric rule worth +0.30 EPDMS as a test-time check on an untrained policy, +0.92 as a training signal, and nothing as a check once it has been trained in. That is one policy, two rules, two candidates and a non-reactive benchmark. The open cases are more candidates, navhard or HUGSIM, and rules the policy cannot absorb from a few hundred examples. See [Inference-Time Safety](wiki/concepts/inference-time-safety.md)

## Workflow

The ingest workflow is defined in `CLAUDE.md`. Each paper goes through:
1. Read source + figures
2. Discuss key takeaways and limitations
3. Create/update `wiki/sources/<paper>.md` with embedded figures and full tables
4. Create/update relevant `wiki/concepts/` pages
5. Check `wiki/research-directions.md` against the paper and update its open questions
6. Update `wiki/index.md` and append to `wiki/log.md`
