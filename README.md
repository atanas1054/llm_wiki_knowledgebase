# LLM-Powered AD Research Wiki

A structured knowledge base for end-to-end autonomous driving research, built by ingesting academic papers with an LLM assistant (Claude). Each paper is read, discussed, and distilled into cross-linked wiki pages covering source summaries and reusable concept notes.

## Structure

```
raw/
  papers/      # Source papers (markdown, converted from arXiv HTML)
  assets/      # Figures extracted from papers
wiki/
  sources/     # One page per paper — summary, figures, tables, limitations
  concepts/    # Cross-paper concept notes (updated as new papers are ingested)
  index.md     # Master catalog of all wiki pages
  log.md       # Append-only ingest log
CLAUDE.md      # Workflow instructions for the LLM assistant
```

## At a Glance

- **85 papers** ingested into `wiki/sources/`, **38 concept pages** in `wiki/concepts/`
- Dominant benchmark is **NAVSIM** (v1 PDMS / v2 EPDMS); also Bench2Drive, nuPlan, nuScenes, WOD-E2E, HUGSIM, PhysicalAI-AV, AlpaSim
- Current NAVSIM-v1 leaders (single-pass, non-BoN): **Drive-HWM 93.8 ⚠ *or* 93.3** > **CLEAR 93.7 = DA-WAM 93.7** > DriveSuprim 93.5 > **[MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) 93.4** (navtrain; **94.0** with the val split added to training, the highest non-BoN number here) > Drive-JEPA 93.3 > **WCog-VLA 92.9 (2B)** > HybridDriveVLA 92.1 (ensemble) > **LWDrive 92.0 (no RL)** ≈ **[DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) 92.0 (video WAM; 91.96 single pass)** > WA-JEPA 91.8 > DynVLA 91.7 > SimWAM 91.5
- **A paper can disagree with itself about its own score.** [Drive-HWM](wiki/sources/drive-hwm.md) reports **93.8 PDMS / 86.4 EPDMS in every table and 93.3 / 86.2 in every sentence** — deciding both whether it leads the list above and whether its own named mechanism is worth +0.8 or +0.3. The wiki records both and ranks it nowhere. See [NAVSIM Benchmark](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md)
- Highest NAVSIM-v2 EPDMS: **WA-JEPA 91.7**, then **[MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) 91.5** (corrected, stated; 64 scored proposals, trainval model), then **SUV 91.0**, then **[ReDrive](wiki/sources/redrive.md) 90.8** (corrected protocol, no scorer; ReDrive predicts nothing at inference). [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) reports 91.6 but states no evaluator and prints no sub-scores, so it is not placed — and **v2 EPDMS is not currently comparable across papers.** Two papers score Transfuser at 76.7 and 84.0 from *identical* submetrics. See [Evaluator Drift](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md). Best-of-6 ceiling: **Curious-VLA 94.8 PDMS** (= human GT)
- Reactive/OOD leader: **DriveFuture 55.5 EPDMS navhard** — but **+20.9 of that is a trajectory scorer and +3.7 is the world model it is named for.** Every navhard entry above 42 scores its own candidates; every entry below 35 does not. The previous leader here, GeoWAM 36.6, tops a table containing no scoring method at all. See [Navhard and OOD Evaluation](wiki/concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md)
- Closed-loop HUGSIM leader: **WA-JEPA 0.4462 HD-Score** zero-shot — on a scenario set and controller version that make earlier HUGSIM numbers in this wiki incomparable. [PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md) is the second method on the 436-scenario set (35.5, commit unstated, WA-JEPA uncited): ahead on Easy, 25 points behind on Medium. [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) is a third (32.3, also unpinned). Its table also shows, by weighting arithmetic, that Latent-WAM's 28.9 was scored on the 436-scenario set and not on the older 345-scenario release where this wiki had filed it. See [HUGSIM](wiki/concepts/hugsim-benchmark.md)
- **Baseline rows are not commensurable by default.** There are *three* EPDMS lineages, not two: [Hydra-MDP++](wiki/sources/hydra-mdp-pp.md)'s own formula (C instead of HC, strict lane keeping, /22), whose rows ReCogDrive, SGDrive and WCog-VLA copy into v2 tables; the pre-fix NAVSIM-v2 evaluator; and the corrected one. At least eleven ingested NAVSIM-v2 tables mix evaluator conventions (SUV's is captioned "corrected" yet carries a pre-fix row; Metis pairs pre-fix anchors with a corrected-looking own row, per the [residual-sign heuristic](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md)), and the failure modes now include *non-headline configurations* cited unmarked (BrainWAM lists DynVLA’s SFT-only score against its published 91.7), *cross-version submetric contamination* (WCog-VLA’s v2 TransFuser row reuses its own v1 submetrics), *oracle Best-of-N scores in single-sample tables* (WCog-VLA lists AutoVLA at its 92.12 BoN rather than 89.11), and *rows with no attributable origin* ([CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) publishes NAVSIM-v2 scores for three methods whose own papers report none), and *ablation rows indistinguishable from external published rows* ([Drive-HWM](wiki/sources/drive-hwm.md): two of four Table IV values equal DriveVLA-W0's two published rows, one of them oracle Best-of-6). The newest mode is *a v1 score assembled from v2 sub-scores* ([PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md), the ninth mixing table and the eighth user of the shared block). [MomWorld](wiki/sources/momworld.md) is the ninth user of that block, and [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md)'s table is captioned "corrected" yet prints DiffusionDriveV2 at its pre-fix 85.5. The arithmetic checks also catch plain errors: [WALT](wiki/sources/walt.md)'s table prints a PRIX row with DAC 85.6 where 95.6 is the only value consistent with its score. Check submetrics and source configuration before comparing any two rows. **The shared v2 baseline block is now confirmed to be copied rather than recomputed** — Drive-HWM is its seventh user and prints the DriveSuprim row under the name "DriveSupervisor" while citing DriveSuprim's own arXiv entry. **Three papers now tabulate both columns instead of mixing them**, but two of them are the same lab — [WA-JEPA](wiki/sources/wa-jepa.md) and [CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) share ten of eleven authors and a GitHub org, and **they still place DriveVLA-W0's 86.1 in different columns.** The rule tightens to: trust a column label only for the paper's own row.
- **The representation is worth several PDMS — but mostly when the planner is weak.** [Unified Driving Tokens](wiki/sources/unified-driving-tokens.md) moves NAVSIM 85.5 → 91.8 by changing only a tokenizer's objectives under a fixed 20M readout; [DriveLaW](wiki/sources/drivelaw.md) spans 84.1 → 89.1 across representation families under a fixed planner. [CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) supplies the wiki's **first complete representation × planner 2×2** and finds they are largely **substitutes** (+5.0 / +5.2 alone, +1.1 / +1.3 together; interaction −3.9). Both prior numbers were measured in the cell where the other factor is weak. See [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
- **Two rules now govern world-model supervision, and they are orthogonal.** *Objective form* follows target entropy — deterministic regression fails in proportion to it ([WA-JEPA](wiki/sources/wa-jepa.md), [DriveFuture](wiki/sources/drivefuture.md)). *Target content* follows the horizon — [Drive-HWM](wiki/sources/drive-hwm.md) ablates the prediction target separately for its 8-step and 1-step branches in one codebase and the ordering fully inverts: **optical flow is best far out, RGB is best one step ahead.** Nobody has crossed the two rules. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **Generation quality and planning quality are decoupled.** [ReWorld](wiki/sources/reworld.md) improves nuScenes FVD 81.3 → 61.9 and NAVSIM PDMS 89.1 → 90.4 in one paper, via *disjoint* mechanisms — and never measures whether the video-side one helps planning. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- **Almost everything above 92.0 PDMS scores its own candidates.** Of the highest non-BoN NAVSIM-v1 entries, all but one train a scorer or an RL reward against the benchmark's own metric. The exception is [Drive-HWM](wiki/sources/drive-hwm.md) (no scorer, no RL), whose own paper cannot decide between 93.8 and 93.3. The recipe traces back to [Hydra-MDP++](wiki/sources/hydra-mdp-pp.md): offline simulation of a fixed vocabulary with ground-truth perception, one distilled head per sub-score, and a grid-searched weighted cost that is its single largest ablation effect (+1.5 PDMS). The two newest entries above 92 fit the pattern in different ways: [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) trains a scorer on simulator sub-scores, and [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) trains its generator on pairs labelled by the evaluator's own collision and drivable-area gates. See [Selection-Based Planning](wiki/concepts/selection-based-planning.md)
- **Nobody has measured training-seed variance on NAVSIM.** All three reported figures are *sampler*-seed noise — [WA-JEPA](wiki/sources/wa-jepa.md) 0.053 over 10 seeds, [CoWorld-VLA](wiki/sources/coworld-vla.md) 0.013 over 6, and [PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md) **0.24–0.30**, so sampler noise is model-specific and spans a factor of twenty — and CoWorld-VLA is the first paper here to say so: variance over independently trained models "was not conducted because of the high training cost." Its own bill was ~7,300 A800-GPU-hours. **Scene-sampling noise is now measured for paired comparisons**: [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) reports bootstrap intervals of about ±0.21 EPDMS for the difference between two checkpoints on full navtest
- **A published planning number is a pipeline number.** [DriveFuture](wiki/sources/drivefuture.md) is the first paper here to report the same checkpoint with and without a scorer: 30.9 → 34.6 (its mechanism) → **55.5** (GTRS-Dense over 100 proposals). Most papers report the pipeline while describing the generator
- **The first closed-loop reproduction of NAVSIM leaders inverts the ranking.** [Qwen-Drive-1.0](wiki/sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md) re-runs [SimWAM](wiki/sources/simwam.md) (91.5 PDMS, the wiki's best video-prior world-action model) and [DriveWAM](wiki/sources/drivewam.md) in **AlpaSim**, a reactive simulator over 916 reconstructed real-log scenarios: SimWAM is **last** on at-fault score (0.30) and Alpamayo-R1, which reports no NAVSIM number at all, is **first** (0.58). DriveWAM's flattering 0.53 comes from **35% progress** — it barely moves, and is struck from behind in 56% of scenarios. See [AlpaSim](wiki/concepts/alpasim-benchmark.md)
- **Driving adaptation barely reaches the planner.** The same paper trains a matched planner on three backbones — unadapted Qwen3.5-4B, +driving VQA, +3D perception — and the **entire adaptation is worth +0.08 RFS** (7.88 → 7.91 → 7.96), against +0.50 for RL on the action head alone, while the same supervision moves driving QA +6.55 and causal reasoning +38.39. **Driving knowledge and driving competence are separate acquisitions.** Sixth consecutive paper where the named mechanism is the smaller term
- **A driving VLM can keep its general capability, and almost none do.** Across 15 general benchmarks and 13 models under one protocol, Qwen-Drive scores **66.41 against its base model's 67.40** while Alpamayo-1.5 scores **14.65**, MiMo-Embodied **26.53** and UniDriveVLA **48.73** — and the *unadapted* base model beats every driving-specialized model on driving QA. The distinguishing variable looks like the **data mixture** (26% general-purpose), not the architecture. See [General-Capability Retention](wiki/concepts/general-capability-retention.md)
- **Vision-language pretraining does not contain driving 3D structure.** A converged BEV head on **frozen** Qwen-initialized features trails a dedicated detector on the *same* features by 6.34 mAP; unfreezing recovers +10.46. Third backbone family to price off-the-shelf use, after a video DiT (−6.42 PDMS) and DINOv2 geometric distillation (LoRA collapses). See [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
- **Three papers, one backbone, four attention masks, and every effect lives on navhard.** [SimWAM](wiki/sources/simwam.md), [Metis](wiki/sources/metis.md) and [SUV](wiki/sources/suv.md) share Wan2.2-5B, a ~1B action expert and nearly the same recipe, and differ mainly in who may attend to whom. On navtest every non-bidirectional mask lands within 0.5 EPDMS. On navhard:
  - Letting the future read the action is worth **+2.2** (Metis).
  - Letting the action read the imagined future is worth **+4.1** (SUV), against +0.3 on navtest.
  - The bidirectional mask is **worst** (Metis), which conflicts with SUV unless the damage is the feedback loop.
  - Outside that family, [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) finds bidirectional the best cell on navtest, but only with modality-specific branches (+0.4; −0.1 in a single DiT). The mask interacts with the block design.

  See [Attention Masks in World-Action Models](wiki/concepts/wam-attention-masks.md)
- **Grounded reasoning helps, but only after RL.** [GRAVA](wiki/sources/grava.md) gives the wiki's most graded CoT ablation. With every variant RL-trained, closed-loop score on its internal long-tail benchmark goes: action-only 74.8 → coarse reasoning 77.9 → box-grounded objects 85.5 → full grounded reasoning 90.1. Without RL, full reasoning scores 73.1, below action-only *with* RL. Editing the trace edits the action (0.44 → 0.82 normalized PDMS), but the trace's final maneuver is copied from the expert trajectory at annotation time. See [Chain-of-Thought for AD](wiki/concepts/chain-of-thought-for-ad.md)
- **The sign of a row's submetric residual appears to reveal its EPDMS protocol.** Recomputing EPDMS from a row's own submetrics and subtracting the reported value gives **+1 to +4 for pre-fix rows and slightly negative for corrected rows**. It flips exactly on the two rows known in both conventions (Human Agent +4.1 / −0.1; DriveFine +1.2 / −0.8). Four exceptions, so it is a heuristic. See [Residual sign](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md)
- **A world model that is shown the future still loses to a policy that is not.** [AD-E2E-JEPA](wiki/sources/ad-e2e-jepa.md) trains no policy: it rolls a JEPA world model out over 256–8,192 vocabulary trajectories and keeps the one closest to the *ground-truth future frame*. That oracle-goal search recovers **67.3–72.9 EPDMS** on the full navtest, against **85.4** for the same paper's goal-free imitation model and about 94.5 for the human trajectory it is aiming at. The scores are a world-model diagnostic and stay off the leaderboard. The paper also shows a 100-scene subset ordering its own four variants differently from the full 12,146. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md) and [Evaluation Variance](wiki/concepts/evaluation-variance.md)
- **A geometric loss can be worth the same on navtest and navhard, and a "v1" score can be built from v2 sub-scores.** [PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md) co-denoises video, metric depth and ego motion in a 15.2B Cosmos 3 model and ties depth to motion through a LiDAR point loss: **+1.9 EPDMS on navtest, +1.9 on navhard, +2.1 HD-Score on HUGSIM**, the first mechanism here that is not several times larger on navhard. Generating depth alone is a null. The paper concedes the coupling itself is not isolated. Its printed **"PDMS 91.4" is kept off the v1 ladder**: all four of its rows carry identical sub-scores under both protocols and a PDMS equal to their closed form, which no other method in its table does. It also measures a **recorded-versus-recorded FVD floor of 91.5** at 600 clips, which makes the wiki's unqualified NAVSIM FVD numbers unrankable, and a **sampler sd of 0.24–0.30**, 5–20× earlier figures. 9.4 GPU-seconds per plan. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md) and [NAVSIM Benchmark](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md)
- **An encoder and a planner are enough for 90.8 EPDMS, and the world model that shaped them is worth +0.7.** [ReDrive](wiki/sources/redrive.md) trains a V-JEPA 2 encoder and a 103M flow-matching planner with a trajectory-conditioned JEPA future predictor that is discarded at inference. Its own ablation: V-JEPA 2 over DINOv2 **+5.5**, unfreezing the encoder **+3.5**, future prediction **+0.7**, planner adaptation through the frozen predictor **+0.3**. It is 0.9 EPDMS behind WA-JEPA, which generates futures at inference with the same encoder family, and it is the best no-access model on navhard (34.4). Two cross-paper consequences: **L1 regression on future latents helps here where WA-JEPA found it harmful** (so the entropy rule needs "unless action-conditioned or never consumed"), and **fine-tuning an image encoder under a predictive loss does not close the gap to V-JEPA 2** (DINOv2 84.4 vs 89.9). Its v2 table is WA-JEPA's corrected column with WA-JEPA removed. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md) and [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
- **An ablation table can be too clean, and a leaderboard can be missing its authors' own paper.** [MomWorld](wiki/sources/momworld.md) adds a momentum variable to a latent world model and feeds a shared future memory to a GTRS-Dense trajectory scorer: 90.2 PDMS, 90.1 EPDMS, **42.8 navhard, which is +1.1 over its base** with Stage-2 progress up 12 points and NC / TTC down 5–6. Its navhard table reuses [DriveFuture](wiki/sources/drivefuture.md)'s baseline rows and omits all six entries above 42.8, including DriveFuture (55.5), with which it shares three authors. Its three ablation tables contain columns that are exact functions of each other (L2@6 − TPC@6 constant to sd 0.008 over 27 rows; NAVSIM PDMS linear in nuScenes L2 across two different base models), so the wiki records the page at **low confidence** and cites no component effect from it. The "long-horizon" gain over MomAD is 34–39% at 1–2 s and 6% at 6 s. See [Evaluation Variance](wiki/concepts/evaluation-variance.md) and [Navhard](wiki/concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md)
- **On a strong frozen world model, the planner's output space is a small lever.** [WALT](wiki/sources/walt.md) freezes EponaV2 and changes only what its trajectory head generates. Raw waypoints, a learned trajectory latent, JEPA-style trajectory pretraining and REPA-style alignment to a trajectory encoder all land within 0.07 PDMS (89.42–89.49). Aligning the latent to the frozen world model's features gives **+0.35** (89.83 PDMS; 87.9 EPDMS, +0.6, mostly extended comfort). That matches [ReWorld](wiki/sources/reworld.md)'s +0.4 for a different alignment on a different backbone and [ReDrive](wiki/sources/redrive.md)'s +0.3 for planner adaptation through a frozen predictor: **three post-hoc ways of coupling a planner to a fixed predictive model, each worth a few tenths.** RL on the same EponaV2 base is worth about +1.0 PDMS / +1.6 EPDMS. See [Action Tokenization](wiki/concepts/action-tokenization.md) and [Diffusion Planner](wiki/concepts/diffusion-planner.md)
- **A safety rule is worth more as a training signal than as a test-time check, and a computed verdict is not worse than a learned one.** [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) re-implements NAVSIM's collision and drivable-area gates as a zero-parameter rule and uses it twice on a one-camera Cosmos 3 video world-action model. On ground-truth maps it builds 462 winner/loser pairs for a preference loss: **+0.92 EPDMS** (95% CI +0.71 to +1.13), to 91.96 PDMS / 91.61 EPDMS in a single pass. On maps predicted from the camera it gates a second sample: **+0.30** on the base policy and **nothing** after the distillation. At two candidates no learned verifier is detectably better than the rule, including one given the same predicted map (−0.01, CI ±0.15), and gating matters more than the verdict source. The rule is itself lossy (63% recall on drivable-area violations even with ground-truth maps). See [Inference-Time Safety](wiki/concepts/inference-time-safety.md) and [Selection-Based Planning](wiki/concepts/selection-based-planning.md)
- **Sixty-four futures, and the future is still the small term.** [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md) generates 64 trajectory–future pairs jointly in 233 ms and scores each trajectory against its own future: 93.4 PDMS on navtrain, 94.0 on trainval, 91.5 corrected EPDMS. Its ablation prices each part: one trajectory 84.1; 32 trajectories with a simulator-trained, history-only scorer **92.3 (+8.2)**; generating a paired future **+0.6**; letting the scorer read it **+0.4**. That is the second per-candidate-future result after [DA-WAM](wiki/sources/da-wam.md) (+0.15), same sign and same size class. On v2 its ego progress (92.2) is 4.8 above the human agent's while driving direction, lane keeping and history comfort are the lowest in its table, because its scorer learned the v1 components. See [Selection-Based Planning](wiki/concepts/selection-based-planning.md) and [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
- Every leaderboard claim in this wiki carries a **comparison-scope caveat** — most papers omit the actual frontier from their tables. See [NAVSIM Benchmark](wiki/concepts/navsim-benchmark.md).

## Open Threads

Questions the wiki has surfaced but not resolved, in rough order of how much they'd change the picture:

1. **Does test-time future imagination help at all? It now depends on the benchmark.** On navtest, three results say no. [SimWAM](wiki/sources/simwam.md)'s mask ablation loses nothing without future access (90.3 vs. 90.2); [DriveLaW](wiki/sources/drivelaw.md) finds early denoising latents beat near-clean futures; [SUV](wiki/sources/suv.md) finds access worth only +0.3. **On navhard, SUV measures +4.1 for the same access**, and [Metis](wiki/sources/metis.md) finds the bidirectional variant worst. The deciding experiment is SimWAM's isolated checkpoint scored on navhard, plus the missing one-way/one-way cell of the mask family. **The one configuration that is positive on navtest is a future per candidate, read by a scorer**: +0.15 in [DA-WAM](wiki/sources/da-wam.md) and +0.4 in [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md), both small and both single runs. See [World Models for AD](wiki/concepts/world-model-for-ad.md)
2. **Is NAVSIM-v1 saturated?** Best-of-6 reaches 94.8 = the human ground-truth score (Curious-VLA). If oracle selection already matches the logged human, single-sample gains above ~93 may be measuring selection quality rather than driving quality. See [Best-of-N Sampling](wiki/concepts/best-of-n.md).
3. **Are video-prior gains about scale or objective?** SimWAM shows video-prior scale barely matters on navtest (Wan2.1-1.3B 90.2 ≈ Wan2.2-5B 90.3), and DriveWAM shows dropping the video objective is catastrophic. **But [Metis](wiki/sources/metis.md) finds the same 1.3B-vs-5B swap roughly equal on navtest and −2.4 on navhard.** Scale may matter exactly where navtest cannot see it. See [Foundation Backbones](wiki/concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md)
4. **Do PDMS gains transfer to closed-loop? The first multi-method answer is no.** Most 90+ PDMS methods still report no Bench2Drive, HUGSIM or navhard result, and stage-2 navhard remains near 40 EPDMS for everything measured. [Qwen-Drive-1.0](wiki/sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md) now supplies the first head-to-head check: in **AlpaSim**, a reactive simulator over 916 reconstructed real-log scenarios, the at-fault ranking is **Alpamayo-R1 0.58 > DriveWAM 0.53 > Alpamayo-1.5 0.45 > Qwen-Drive-RL 0.37 > SimWAM 0.30**, while the NAVSIM ranking of the same methods runs **SimWAM 91.5 > Qwen-Drive 90.7 > DriveWAM 90.1** with both Alpamayo variants reporting no NAVSIM number at all. The two best closed-loop methods are not on the leaderboard and the best leaderboard method is last. Neither WAM behaves well for the reason its paper predicts: DriveWAM is degenerately cautious (35% progress, 56% all-event close encounters) and SimWAM degenerately aggressive (22% at-fault, 19% off-road). These are third-party reproductions at one run each, and the AlpaSim score is a ratio that flatters near-stationary policies — but a 1.4-point PDMS lead carried no closed-loop information at all. The same paper also finds that **RL shaped against PDMS moves closed-loop behaviour in the opposite direction from how it moves PDMS**: NAVSIM ego progress 82.4 → 84.8, AlpaSim progress 54% → 48%, with off-road halved. See [AlpaSim](wiki/concepts/alpasim-benchmark.md).
5. **Is navtest measuring the wrong thing for world models? The evidence is now systematic.** Four independent navtest-small / navhard-large effects: [GeoWAM](wiki/sources/geowam.md) future geometry +0.6 / +4.9; SUV future access +0.3 / +4.1; Metis asymmetric mask +0.5 / +2.2; Metis video-prior scale ≈0 / +2.4. Caveat: the navhard split size is itself disputed (244 vs. 450 Stage-1 scenes; two papers now say 450). **First exception**: [PhysWAM](wiki/sources/physwam.md)'s depth–motion loss is worth +1.9 on navtest and +1.9 on navhard, so the asymmetry belongs to inference-path mechanisms (access, masks, prior scale), not to every world-model addition. Originally: GeoWAM adds future-geometry forecasting to DVGT-2 and gains **+0.6 EPDMS on navtest but +4.9 on navhard** — the same change worth eight times more under the reactive protocol, exactly as a world-model thesis predicts. Every world-model paper in the wiki optimizes navtest; only two report navhard at all. If the asymmetry replicates, the field has been evaluating anticipation on a benchmark where anticipation barely pays.
6. **Is reasoning a rationale or a verbalized action?** [GRAVA](wiki/sources/grava.md) shows the action follows the trace, but its trace ends in a maneuver copied from the expert trajectory. No paper yet builds the final decision only from grounded object-level evidence and tests whether the ladder survives. See [Chain-of-Thought for AD](wiki/concepts/chain-of-thought-for-ad.md)
7. **Comparison-scope inflation is systemic.** Nearly every ingested paper claims SOTA against a table that omits the actual frontier. The wiki tracks this per-paper, but it makes cross-paper ranking unreliable in principle.
8. **What is a detectable effect on navtest?** [DriveReferee](wiki/sources/drivereferee.md) supplies the first paired, per-scene confidence intervals: about ±0.2 EPDMS for the difference between two checkpoints, before any training-seed variance, which nobody has measured. Many of the mechanism effects recorded here are 0.3–0.6 from single runs ([WALT](wiki/sources/walt.md) +0.4, [MM-Future](wiki/sources/mm-future.md)'s pairing +0.6 and future-aware scoring +0.4, ReDrive's future prediction +0.7). And an ablation table can fail in the other direction: [MomWorld](wiki/sources/momworld.md)'s columns are exact functions of each other. See [Evaluation Variance](wiki/concepts/evaluation-variance.md)
9. **Should a safety rule be checked at inference or trained in?** DriveReferee finds the same geometric rule worth +0.30 EPDMS as a test-time check on an untrained policy, +0.92 as a training signal, and nothing as a check once it has been trained in. That is one policy, two rules, two candidates and a non-reactive benchmark. The open cases are more candidates, navhard or HUGSIM, and rules the policy cannot absorb from a few hundred examples. See [Inference-Time Safety](wiki/concepts/inference-time-safety.md)
