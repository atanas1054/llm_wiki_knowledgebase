## What It Is

NAVSIM is a planning-oriented autonomous driving benchmark built on OpenScene (a redistribution of nuPlan). It provides a **non-reactive simulation** environment for closed-loop planning evaluation.

## Dataset

- Built on **OpenScene** / **nuPlan**
- **Sensors**: 8 cameras (1920×1080) + fused LiDAR point cloud from 5 sensors across current + 3 prior frames
- **Splits**: navtrain (1,192 training scenes), navtest (136 evaluation scenes)
- 85,109 trajectory-based QA pairs in navtrain

## Primary Metric: PDMS

**Predictive Driver Model Score**:
$$\text{PDMS} = NC \times DAC \times \frac{5 \times EP + 5 \times TTC + 2 \times C}{12}$$

Sub-metrics:
| Metric | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| NC | No At-Fault Collision |
| DAC | Drivable Area Compliance |
| EP | Ego Progress |
| TTC | Time-to-Collision |
| C (Comf.) | Comfort |

NC and DAC are multiplicative gates — a collision zeroes the score.

## Extended Metrics (Hydra-MDP++)

- **TL**: Traffic Light Compliance
- **LK**: Lane Keeping Ability
- **DDC**: Driving Direction Compliance
- **EC**: Extended Comfort
- **EPDMS**: Extended PDMS incorporating the above

## Non-Reactive Simulator

NAVSIM's simulator does not model other agents reacting to the ego vehicle. This simplifies evaluation (and RL training) but means scores may not reflect performance in fully interactive scenarios.

## PaIR-Drive: Human-Bad Splits and Metric Tradeoffs

[[sources/pair-drive.md]] derives two diagnostic subsets from navtest: `human bad v1` contains scenarios with human PDMS below 85, and `human bad v2` contains scenarios with human EPDMS below 80. Refining the human trajectory raises 82.3→83.9 PDMS on v1 and 50.0→60.8 EPDMS on v2.

These subsets show that the recorded human path is not an oracle ceiling under PDM scoring. They also expose why aggregate scores must be decomposed: on `human bad v2`, PaIR-Drive improves direction, traffic-light, progress, and lane-keeping terms but lowers Extended Comfort from 56.6 to 49.3. With DiffusionDrive on navtest v2, PaIR-Drive raises EPDMS 84.3→87.9 while EC falls 87.6→74.0.

For leaderboard use, separate PaIR-Drive's single-plan 91.2 PDMS / 87.9 EPDMS from its Best-of-6 94.0 / 89.6. The latter is a proposal-and-selection system, not a directly comparable single-output planner.

World model bypassed at inference. See [[sources/drivevla-w0.md]].

**Caveat on DriveDreamer-Policy (89.2)**: Table 1 compares within "world-model-based" category vs. LAW, DrivingGPT, WoTE, Epona, FSDrive, PWM, plus VLA methods AutoVLA (89.1), DriveVLA-W0 (88.4), and ReCogDrive *IL-only* (86.5). **The 92.6 is a different model and does not belong on this table's single-trajectory ladder.** It is a fixed block-22 exit with **64 proposals**, no adaptive controller, a non-diffusion four-block refinement decoder, and **CLOVER-derived pseudo-expert targets scored with the true NAVSIM evaluator using training-time map and future occupancy**. That is privileged supervision of the same class flagged for Hydra-MDP distillation, Auto-JEPA, and DA-WAM. Compare it against [[concepts/selection-based-planning.md]] entries — DriveSuprim 93.5, CLEAR 93.7 — where it does not lead. Within its own table 89.9 sits **0.4 below the Human Agent's 90.3**, the closest approach to the human reference recorded here on v2; EC 75.5 is the weak sub-score.

**What the paper is actually worth is diagnostic, not positional.** It separates two axes this wiki had been conflating and measures both: the **video noise index is worth ≤0.15 PDMS** across five indices of a 40-step schedule, while **readout depth is worth 4.80** — the mid-network block 15 beats the full-depth block 30, with all six exits sharing architecture and training budget. It is also the **second paper here to run a multi-seed protocol** (validation-best per seed over ten seeds), though that is a selection rule rather than a variance report, and no PDMS standard deviation is given. And it is by a wide margin the **fastest world-action model on this page: 170 ms end-to-end on an A100**, against SimWAM 518 ms, BrainWAM 475–644 ms, and ForeSight 900 ms. Its own routing gain over a fixed exit is only +0.17 PDMS; the latency result is the defensible one. No navhard, Bench2Drive, or HUGSIM; front camera only; code not yet released. See [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]].

**Caveat on GeoWorldAD (91.0 PDMS / 90.4 EPDMS)**: the claim is **correctly scoped and the v1 baseline hygiene is the best in recent ingests**. The paper says "best performance among **perception-free** methods on NAVSIM v1" — iPad sits in the same table at 91.7 and beats it, but uses map and box supervision. Transfuser 84.0, UniAD 83.4, PARA-Drive 84.0, VADv2 80.9, DiffusionDrive 88.1, WoTE 88.3, Epona 86.2, and DriveLaW 89.1 all match this page's canonical values, and it cites **DriveVLA-W0 at its 90.2 headline** rather than the 87.2 reimplementation row that DriveLaW, BrainWAM, and Adaptive-WAM all propagate. DriveSuprim appears at 89.9, the widely-circulated non-ViT-L figure rather than 93.5.

In the broader wiki 91.0 sits below CLEAR/DA-WAM 93.7, DriveSuprim 93.5, Drive-JEPA 93.3, HybridDriveVLA 92.1, WA-JEPA 91.8, and DynVLA 91.7. Note also that the headline uses **64 proposals with a scorer trained on the NAVSIM simulator's own PDMS composition**, so it belongs in the [selection-based](selection-based-planning.md) family and is not a single-trajectory result.

**The v2 result is the more interesting one, and it partially repairs a standing problem.** 90.4 EPDMS leads its own table, with EP 89.1 and TTC 98.6 the best rows and EC 82.2 respectable for a world-model method. More importantly, GeoWorldAD reports **DVGT-2 at 89.6 and EponaV2 at 88.9 — identical to GeoWAM's values — while giving Transfuser 76.7 and DiffusionDrive 84.5** where GeoWAM gives 84.0 and 88.2. See [GeoWorldAD Narrows the GeoWAM Anomaly](#geowam-narrowed): the anomaly is now best read as two bad rows in GeoWAM's table rather than a third protocol, and the two geometry papers are comparable to each other. GeoWorldAD's own table still mixes conventions, so it is the fifth to do so.

**The ablation to treat carefully.** Its future-geometry module is worth +1.7 PDMS / +2.8 EPDMS over the present-geometry-only planner (GeoAD) — the largest positive shared-future result in this wiki — but **GeoAD has 32K planner steps and GeoWorldAD has 96K**, so the mechanism is confounded with 3× the training budget. The **coordinate-frame ablation is the clean one**: re-expressing the same StreamVGGT's point maps in per-timestep ego frames, with no added capacity, is worth **+2.5 PDMS** (84.8 → 87.3), and an anchor-frame geometry model barely beats training from scratch (84.8 vs 84.2) while *lowering* NC, DAC, and TTC.

No navhard, HUGSIM, Bench2Drive, or nuScenes planning; no latency, FPS, or parameter count; single runs; 32× H20 across four training stages. See [[sources/geoworldad.md]].

**Caveat on WCog-VLA (92.9 PDMS / 85.9 EPDMS)**: the v1 result is the **fourth-highest in this wiki** — behind CLEAR/DA-WAM 93.7, DriveSuprim 93.5, and Drive-JEPA 93.3, ahead of HybridDriveVLA 92.1 — and it is achieved with a **2B** backbone, making it the strongest PDMS-per-parameter entry tracked here. Its comparison against ReCogDrive-2B (90.8) is genuinely like-for-like. NC 99.4 and TTC 98.5 are both second-highest in the wiki (behind WA-JEPA 99.5 and DriveVA 98.7); DAC 98.8 trails only DriveVLA-W0's 99.1.

**One baseline row is an oracle result presented as a single-sample one.** Its table lists **AutoVLA-3B at 92.1**, which is AutoVLA's **Best-of-6 oracle selection** (92.12); the published single-sample post-RFT score is **89.11**. The rest of the v1 table matches canonical values (TransFuser 84.0, UniAD 83.4, DiffusionDrive 88.1, WoTE 88.3, iPad 91.7, ReCogDrive-2B 90.8). Two methods new here: LatentVLA-3B 92.4 and BevDrive 83.8.

**What the number is made of.** Its own four-stage ablation gives 84.4 → 85.5 (+3D perception) → 89.3 (+ADDT continuous action head) → **92.9 (+DiffGRPO reinforcement fine-tuning)**. RFT is +3.6 and the diffusion head is +3.8; the dual-level world cognition the paper is named for is worth **+2.8** in the separate Table 4. It is also a heavily supervised system — 3D boxes, per-agent future trajectories, BEV supervision, and 328k VQA/CoT samples.

**The v2 number cannot be placed, and it is a different model.** 85.9 EPDMS is reported after **three-stage SFT with no RFT** — the stage worth +3.6 on v1 — which the caption states but never flags. More seriously, its v2 baselines are a **fourth distinct set**: TransFuser at **77.8** with submetrics matching neither this page's 76.7 row nor GeoWAM's 84.0, and whose NC/DAC/EP/TTC (97.7 / 92.8 / 79.2 / 92.8) are *identical to its own NAVSIM-v1 row in the same paper*, with LK 67.6 against 92.7 everywhere else. HydraMDP++ appears at 80.6 (this page 84.1, BrainWAM 81.4 — a third value) and DiffusionDrive at 84.3 (elsewhere 84.5). ARTEMIS carries HC 100 where every other source gives 98.3, though its EC 98.3 agrees with DA-WAM against BrainWAM's 89.1. Its strongest v2 baseline is DiffusionDrive at 84.3, so "SOTA" there is scoped to a notably weak field.

No navhard, Bench2Drive, HUGSIM, or nuScenes. Single runs, no seed variance. Deployed end-to-end latency is unreported — the 0.106 s figure covers the action head only, and the same table shows Game-CoT text generation at 9.896 s. See [[sources/wcog-vla.md]].

### How Much Does the Seed Matter? (First Measurement)

Nearly every caveat on this page notes that a paper reports one deterministic run with no seed variance. [[sources/wa-jepa.md]] is the first ingested paper to measure it — 10 seeds, model and scenarios fixed, sampling noise reinitialized, full EPDMS recomputed per seed:

| Statistic | EPDMS |
|---|---|
| Mean | 91.7014 |
| Standard deviation | **0.0531** |
| 95% $t$-confidence interval | [91.6634, 91.7393] |
| Range | [91.6294, 91.8070] |

**Seed noise is small**: a 0.05 standard deviation and a 0.18 full range for a stochastic 12-step flow sampler. Two consequences. Ablation deltas above roughly 0.2 EPDMS are meaningful, which retroactively supports most of the ablation reading on this page. And the wiki's habitual worry about single-run NAVSIM numbers is **much less serious than the evaluator-version problem below** — a +2.0 to +3.8 protocol shift dwarfs a ±0.05 seed shift by nearly two orders of magnitude.

Caveats: one measurement, one method, and only the headline experiment — WA-JEPA's own ablations are presumably single-seed, and deterministic planners have a different (probably smaller) noise profile driven by data order rather than sampling.

### Evaluator Drift: This Table Mixes Two Protocols

**This is the most important caveat on the page, and it invalidates naive column-reading of the NAVSIM-v2 table above.**

NAVSIM-v2's EPDMS aggregation was corrected at devkit commit [`359c7f7`](https://github.com/autonomousvision/navsim/commit/359c7f72304bfa8273e754224a213d3751bd2340), which recomputes the multiplicative and weighted terms *after* applying the human-reference penalty filter. That filter sets a sub-metric to 1 when the human reference incurs the same violation, for $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{filt}=\{\mathrm{NC},\mathrm{DAC},\mathrm{DDC},\mathrm{TLC},\mathrm{EP},\mathrm{TTC},\mathrm{LK},\mathrm{HC}\}$ on first-stage scenarios. It does not remove scenarios; it suppresses a metric-specific penalty. Papers describe the same correction under at least three names — "bug-fixed" ([[sources/drivefine.md]]), "updated official implementation with human-behavior filtering" ([[sources/auto-jepa.md]]), and "EPDMS* vs. EPDMS" ([[sources/wa-jepa.md]]).

[[sources/wa-jepa.md]] is the first ingested paper to report both columns for a whole leaderboard, which makes the size of the correction measurable across methods:

| [[sources/drivefine.md]] | 87.1 | 89.7 | +2.6 |

| Discrete-WAM *(not ingested)* | 87.0 | 90.4 | +3.4 |

### GeoWAM Breaks the Two-Protocol Model {#three-protocols}

The clean corrected/pre-fix partition above **does not survive contact with a third paper**. [[sources/geowam.md]] states it reports "the official human-penalty protocol," and produces these:

| Method | Submetrics (NC/DAC/DDC/TLC/EP/TTC/LK/HC/EC) | GeoWAM's EPDMS | Elsewhere |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Transfuser** | 96.9 / 89.9 / 97.8 / 99.7 / 87.1 / 95.4 / 92.7 / 98.3 / 87.2 — **digit-for-digit identical in both papers** | **84.0** | **76.7** (WA-JEPA, Drive-JEPA, and the table above) |
| DiffusionDrive | differ by at most 0.3 on DAC/DDC/EP/LK/HC | **88.2** | **84.5** (WA-JEPA, labelled *corrected*) |

**The tally now stands at four papers to one** (see [GeoWorldAD Narrows the GeoWAM Anomaly](#geowam-narrowed) below). [[sources/da-wam.md]] also reports TransFuser at **76.7**, agreeing with WA-JEPA and Drive-JEPA against GeoWAM's 84.0. That does not resolve which is correct — a majority is not a proof — but it makes GeoWAM's row the outlier rather than the rule.

**Transfuser is the airtight case: nine identical submetrics, two aggregate scores 7.3 points apart.** No single aggregation rule produces both. And GeoWAM's table is not internally consistent either — its DriveSuprim (83.1) and Hydra-MDP++ (81.4) rows are byte-identical to Drive-JEPA's ResNet-34 rows *including* the EPDMS, while Transfuser and DiffusionDrive have been recomputed.

So at least one of the following is true, and published tables cannot tell us which:

1. There are **more than two** EPDMS aggregation variants in circulation.
2. WA-JEPA's corrected/pre-fix labelling of other papers' numbers is partly wrong.
3. Papers are copying baseline rows across sources without recomputing, producing tables that internally mix conventions.

(3) is demonstrably happening in GeoWAM's table regardless of (1) and (2) — **and in DA-WAM's**, which carries TransFuser, SparseDriveV2, DriveSuprim, and ARTEMIS at WA-JEPA's pre-fix values while carrying DiffusionDriveV2 at its corrected value (87.5, against a pre-fix 85.5). DA-WAM's headline claim of exceeding "the strongest comparison by 0.2 points" is therefore measured against the one row in its table drawn from the other convention. It also inherits ARTEMIS's EC as 98.3, duplicating that method's HC, where two other papers report "–".

**WCog-VLA supplies a fourth distinct TransFuser value — 77.8 — and it exposes the mechanism.** Its v2 TransFuser row carries NC 97.7, DAC 92.8, EP 79.2, and TTC 92.8, which are *identical to that method's NAVSIM-**v1** row in the same paper's Table 1*, with v2-only metrics (LK 67.6, HC 100, EC 95.3) appended. LK 67.6 against 92.7 everywhere else is not an aggregation difference; it is a different evaluation. So at least one published v2 table is assembled by taking v1 submetrics and filling in the extended columns from somewhere else. That is a **fifth failure mode**: cross-version submetric contamination. WCog-VLA also gives HydraMDP++ 80.6 (a third value after 84.1 and 81.4) and DiffusionDrive 84.3 (against 84.5).

**BrainWAM is the third table provably mixing conventions**, and it mixes them differently again: TransFuser 76.7, ARTEMIS 83.1, and DriveVLA-W0 86.1 match the pre-fix cohort, while HydraMDP++ 81.4 and DriveSuprim 83.1 match neither cohort (the wiki carries 84.1 and 87.1). Its DriveSuprim submetrics differ from DriveSuprim's published row throughout, so that entry is a different *configuration* rather than a recomputed one — a fourth failure mode beyond the three enumerated above: **a baseline row silently drawn from a non-headline configuration**. BrainWAM's v1 table does this three times over (DynVLA 87.2 = SFT-only against a published 91.7, DriveVLA-W0 87.2 = DriveLaW's † reimplementation, ReCogDrive 86.5 = IL-only). The practical consequence: **submetric agreement, not just the aggregate, has to be checked before treating any two rows as commensurable.**

Two independent tables now provably mix conventions. This is the mechanism by which the confusion propagates: baseline rows are copied from whichever prior paper reported them, and no paper recomputes.

**What this means in practice.** The cohort partition above should be read as *WA-JEPA's attribution*, not as ground truth — it was the best available reading when WA-JEPA was ingested and GeoWAM undermines it. The defensible position is stronger and simpler: **NAVSIM-v2 EPDMS is not currently a comparable number across papers.** A same-method, same-submetric discrepancy of 7.3 points exceeds the entire spread of the wiki's top ten v2 entries. Rankings within a single paper's table remain meaningful; rankings assembled across papers — including the table on this page — do not.

The only reliable fixes are things papers can do and mostly do not: pin the devkit commit (WA-JEPA does), publish submetrics so aggregation can be checked (most do, which is how this was caught), and recompute every baseline rather than copying rows (WA-JEPA does for HUGSIM, nobody does for navtest).

**Practical rule**: an EPDMS number is comparable only to others under the same evaluator. Most papers do not say which they used, and the unclassified row above is larger than either identified cohort — so a single ranked v2 column should be read as indicative, not as a leaderboard. Where a paper reports both, this page now names both.

**Same problem, other benchmark.** [[concepts/hugsim-benchmark.md]] has an independent instance: HUGSIM grew from 345 to 436 scenarios and applied a trajectory-to-heading coordinate-order fix (PR #57), so the wiki's HAD and Latent-WAM HD-Scores are not comparable to WA-JEPA's. Protocol drift is not a NAVSIM-specific quirk; it is what happens to any benchmark under active maintenance, and papers that pin a commit are doing the field a service.

### GeoWorldAD Narrows the GeoWAM Anomaly {#geowam-narrowed}

[[sources/geoworldad.md]] is the first ingested paper to report a NAVSIM-v2 table that overlaps GeoWAM's on the rows that matter, and the overlap is informative.

| Method | GeoWAM | **GeoWorldAD** | Elsewhere in this wiki |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **DVGT-2** | 89.6 | **89.6** ✓ | — (not otherwise reported) |
| **EponaV2** | 88.9 | **88.9** ✓ | — (not otherwise reported) |
| Transfuser | **84.0** | **76.7** | 76.7 (WA-JEPA, Drive-JEPA, DA-WAM) |
| DiffusionDrive | **88.2** | **84.5** | 84.5 (WA-JEPA, Adaptive-WAM) |

**GeoWorldAD reproduces GeoWAM's two headline anchors digit-for-digit while reporting the standard values for the two rows that made GeoWAM look like a third protocol.** That changes the most economical explanation. It is no longer "GeoWAM computed its whole table under a different aggregation"; it is "**GeoWAM's Transfuser and DiffusionDrive rows are anomalous, and the rest of its table is sound**." The tally on Transfuser is now **four papers to one**.

**Two consequences.**

1. **GeoWAM's 90.2 and GeoWorldAD's 90.4 are comparable to each other**, both measured against DVGT-2 at 89.6 and EponaV2 at 88.9. The warning on the GeoWAM page should be read as covering two rows rather than the whole table, and the honest attributions — GeoWAM +0.6 over DVGT-2, GeoWorldAD +0.8 — are directly comparable.
2. **This does not rescue the global partition.** GeoWorldAD's own table pairs a pre-fix Transfuser (76.7) with a corrected DiffusionDrive (84.5), so it is the fifth ingested table shown to mix conventions. The general rule stands: **NAVSIM-v2 EPDMS is not comparable across papers without checking submetrics**, and this is a local resolution about one paper, not a repair of the leaderboard.

It also reinforces explanation (3) above — papers copy baseline rows from whichever source reported them. GeoWAM and GeoWorldAD evidently drew DVGT-2 and EponaV2 from the same place and their Transfuser/DiffusionDrive rows from different ones.

**Curious-VLA NAVSIM-v2 note**: compares against Ego-MLP, VADv2, TransFuser, HydraMDP++, DriveSuprim, ARTEMIS, ReCogDrive, and DiffusionDrive — not Senna-2 (86.6) or DriveDreamer-Policy (88.7). EC = 81.5 vs. DiffusionDrive's 87.7 — exploration creates more aggressive maneuvers. See [[sources/curious-vla.md]].
