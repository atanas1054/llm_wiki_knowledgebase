# Research Directions

The wiki-wide list of open research questions, grouped by theme. Each entry states a question, what the ingested papers say about it so far, and where the detail lives. The "Open Questions" section of each concept page remains the full local record; this page collects the questions that matter across pages.

Every claim traces to a wiki page. The file is checked and updated on every ingest and every lint. Answered questions are moved to [Answered](#answered), not deleted.

Entry format:

```
### The question, as a heading
- **Status**: open | partially answered
- **Known so far**: the evidence to date, with numbers and [[page]] citations
- **See**: [[pages]] that discuss it
- **Updated**: YYYY-MM-DD
```

## 1. Evaluation and benchmark validity

### How large are the error bars on NAVSIM and navhard comparisons?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: Until 2026-10-06 training-seed variance had never been measured. The three earlier figures are sampler-seed noise: 0.053 over 10 seeds ([[sources/wa-jepa.md]]), 0.013 over 6 ([[sources/coworld-vla.md]]) and 0.24–0.30 ([[sources/physwam.md]]). Paired scene-sampling noise is measured once, within one model family: a 95% half-width of about 0.21 EPDMS on navtest ([[sources/drivereferee.md]]). Across two different published methods it is unmeasured, and no paper reports per-scene navhard scores, so no navhard effect in the wiki has an error bar. [[sources/simscale.md]] gives the first look at run-to-run scatter on navhard: separately trained models one data step apart (7–33% more data) reverse by up to 2.7 EPDMS along curves that should be smooth. That is not a training-seed measurement, since the data changes too, but it suggests navhard differences under about 2 points need more than one run. Outside NAVSIM, closed-loop CARLA is noisier still: [[sources/think2drive.md]] reports DS standard deviations of 1, 9 and 12.2 over three runs for three policies on 390 single-scenario routes. **Training-seed variance has now been measured once**: [[sources/foredrive.md]] retrains a 118M single-camera planner from three seeds, 89.7–89.9 PDMS on navtest, sd 0.1. One recipe, three seeds, navtest only. Private closed-loop benchmarks add their own: [[sources/rad.md]]'s 337-clip collision ratio has a binomial SE near 0.02, and the same methods on a second 3DGS benchmark from the same institutions ([[sources/senna2.md]]) keep their order but halve RAD's advantage. [[sources/world4scorer.md]] reports the first log-clustered intervals, bootstrapping over the 136 navtest logs rather than over scenes (a fixed-pool scorer's regret: 5.14 [4.66, 5.62]); its training ablations are single runs. [[sources/hyworldvla.md]] illustrates both halves of the problem. Its named navtest components are worth 0.24–0.42 PDMS each, single runs, near ForeDrive's training-seed sd of 0.1. Its private 655-scene rain/fog subset has a scene-sampling SE of at least about 0.7 PDMS, and two pixel-only models of the same recipe, 0.29 apart on navtest, are 8.77 apart there. [[sources/simvta.md]] gives a second training-seed measurement, and the first for a large model. It repeats four configurations of its RL post-training of Epona (2.5B) from one fixed checkpoint over three seeds: sd 0.06–0.1 PDMS, with the same ordering on every seed (its 0.3-point selection effect holds on all three). Pretraining seeds, navhard and v2 are still unmeasured. A third training-seed measurement and the first in closed loop: [[sources/maneuverflow.md]] trains a ResNet-34 generate–score–refine planner three times, sd 0.04–0.06 on navtest PDMS and EPDMS, and the same three checkpoints spread by 0.64–0.92 PDMS\* on a 288-scenario 3DGS closed-loop benchmark (WorldEngine). Open-loop training-seed sd is now 0.04–0.1 across three recipes; closed-loop margins under about 2 points need several runs. [[sources/drivor.md]] chose its NAVSIM-v2 inference weights, training length, split and targets on warmup-two-stage, which it describes as 7 scenes; its ablations are single runs on navval, with most compression and register effects at 0.2–0.5 PDMS. [[sources/clover.md]] repeats its full training three times: 94.488–94.502 PDMS and 90.354–90.368 EPDMS, a range of 0.014 on both, an order of magnitude below the other three training-seed figures. Which stages each seed re-runs is not stated, and its ablations are single runs on navtest. A second CARLA spread, on the older Town05 Long benchmark: [[sources/law.md]] reports 67.9 ± 2.1 and 70.1 ± 2.6 DS without and with its world-model loss (run count unstated), so the effect it attributes to the loss is smaller than either sd. A selection effect at the top of the v1 ladder: [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]]'s headline (94.85, printed 94.8) is the 12-step point of a denoising-step sweep on navtest (2 / 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 steps: 94.68 / 94.72 / 94.74 / 94.85 / 94.67, default 4), and its three named components are worth +0.3, +0.1 and +0.7 in single runs. [[sources/toad.md]] tunes its search with one planner on warmup-two-stage (7 scenes), where neighbouring settings swing by up to 8.8 EPDMS, then fixes the values for six planners. CEM is stochastic and no sampler-seed spread is reported. Two of its six v1 gains are +0.1.

### Can NAVSIM-v2 EPDMS be compared across papers at all?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: There are three EPDMS lineages (the Hydra-MDP++ formula, the pre-fix evaluator and the corrected one). **Recount at the 2026-10-14 lint: of 53 navtest v2 tables transcribed on the source pages, 37 contain both pre-fix-like and corrected-like rows by residual sign** *(2026-10-19 lint, not recounted: five later ingests added navtest v2 tables. DVGT-2's, RAF-VLA's and DriveWLA's mix conventions, the last under an "all corrected" caption. CLOVER's and SparseDriveV2's print both columns, with labels that agree with the residual sign.)* (35–37 under stricter cut-offs), and only 18 of 397 rows fall in the ambiguous band between −0.2 and +1.0. The residual distribution is bimodal, so the sign classifies nearly every row; mixing is the norm, not the exception ([[concepts/navsim-benchmark.md#v2-recount]]). This replaces the hand count ("at least eleven") that the 84.5 re-reading had put in doubt. Two papers score Transfuser at 76.7 and 84.0 from identical sub-metrics. The residual-sign heuristic (+1 to +4 for pre-fix rows, slightly negative for corrected ones) identifies the protocol of most rows but has exceptions (four until 2026-10-06, three since; see below). The current rule is to trust a column label only for the paper's own row. navhard has its own provenance problems. [[sources/simscale.md]]'s camera-only LTF (24.4) and DiffusionDrive (27.5) baselines are digit-identical to rows Metis labels "copied from GTRS", while its own scaling figure starts LTF at about 27.5. The "SimScale 53.2" row in DriveFuture's navhard table does not appear in the SimScale paper, whose best is 50.9. SimScale's navtest table is pre-fix throughout by residual sign (+1.4 to +2.9) and names no evaluator. [[sources/foredrive.md]]'s v2 table is another unlabelled mix (five pre-fix-like rows, nine corrected-like), and its "+2.2 over Drive-JEPA" crosses evaluators; it also introduces a corrected-like ReCogDrive-IL row at 86.6 beside the shared block's pre-fix 83.6. [[sources/world4scorer.md]]'s v2 table is the most uniformly corrected-like yet (18 of 21 rows with negative residuals). It prints DiffusionDrive at **88.2** on sub-scores within 0.3 of the 84.5 row's, as GeoWAM did without being cited, with a corrected-like residual (−0.9). The simpler reading is now that 84.5 is the pre-fix value, which removes one of the heuristic's four exceptions and reverses the wiki's earlier verdict that GeoWAM's row was the anomaly. The same table prints TransFuser at 83.9 on its own corrected-like sub-scores; new rows for Drive-JEPA (93.7 / 90.8), DrivoR (90.7) and SafeDrive (87.5, residual +4.0, the largest of any planner row); and ReCogDrive and WAM-Flow with EC 27.6 and 33.8 against 87.7 and 73.9 elsewhere. See also the new question on extended comfort below. The re-reading has knock-on effects that the 2026-10-06 lint found unpropagated: [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]]'s and [[sources/geoworldad.md]]'s tables were counted among the mixing tables because their DiffusionDrive 84.5 was taken as corrected. Under the new reading Adaptive-WAM's three named baselines are all pre-fix (its human agent is the pre-fix 90.3), GeoWorldAD's mix rests only on its DVGT-2 and EponaV2 rows, both "corrected" rows in [[sources/lwdrive.md]]'s table carry pre-fix-like residuals, and [[sources/redrive.md]]'s table, captioned corrected, carries one pre-fix row. So the "at least eleven" count of mixing tables needs recounting: up to three tables may drop out and ReDrive's may join. In the shared baseline block, TransFuser, ARTEMIS and DiffusionDrive would all be pre-fix, DriveVLA-W0 86.1 (residual −1.3) stays the odd one, and Hydra-MDP++ 81.4 and DriveSuprim 83.1 match neither cohort. Which source first printed 84.5, and under which evaluator, would settle it. A new failure mode, shifted rows: [[sources/hyworldvla.md]]'s v2 table prints DreamerAD's published row under Latent-WAM's name (87.7, EC 72.4) and DriveWorld-VLA's 86.8 row under DreamerAD's, so Latent-WAM's own 89.3 is missing and the claimed margin over it is +2.0 instead of +0.4. The same table is the tenth recorded user of the shared baseline block in one unlabelled column: the block's TransFuser, HydraMDP++, DriveSuprim, DiffusionDrive and ReCogDrive rows are pre-fix-like (+1.25 to +2.48), while the paper's own row (−0.91) and ExploreVLA (−0.68) are corrected-like. [[sources/idol.md]] extends the residual-sign fingerprint to navhard. In its stage-1-only table every baseline's reported score sits 1.8–7.6 below the closed form of its own sub-scores and IDOL's 0.4 above; by closed form Hydra-MDP (V2-99) leads IDOL 80.4 to 75.9, reversing the claimed +3.1. Its two-stage table shows the same split more weakly (product of stage closed forms: baselines 0.2–2.2 above the reported score, IDOL 2.6 below). Its navtest v2 table is another mix: IDOL (−0.60) and MeanFuser (−0.90) corrected-like, the pre-fix human agent (90.3) and five of the shared block's six rows pre-fix-like, plus an unidentified "VADv2 85.8" row. [[sources/simvta.md]] states its protocol (NAVSIM v2.2 devkit, single-stage, LQR, non-reactive) for its own row and its re-evaluated Epona, and both land on the corrected side (−0.76, −1.70), as do its DreamerAD and SGDrive rows; ResAD, DriveSuprim, DiffRefiner, DriveWorld-VLA, ELF-VLA and SafeDrive are pre-fix-like (+2.6 to +4.0). That is a stated row agreeing with the fingerprint. By closed form five of the six pre-fix-like rows sit above SimVTA, so the table does not show where it ranks on one scale. [[sources/maneuverflow.md]]'s v2 table is captioned "a single official benchmark version" and still mixes conventions (TransFuser 76.7 and DriveSuprim 83.1 pre-fix-like; DiffusionDrive 88.3, MeanFuser 89.5 and its own rows corrected-like). It also prints an ICLR 2026 VADv2 at 89.3 PDMS v1, which plausibly pairs with IDOL's unexplained "VADv2 85.8" v2 row: two different models now circulate under one name. *(Corrected 2026-10-14: three. DA-WAM's and DrivoR's tables carry VAD-v2 at 86.2, DA-WAM's also labelled ICLR'26, so which value is the ICLR 2026 paper's, and which pairs with 85.8, is open.)* **On navhard, the offset is now measured.** [[sources/drivor.md]]'s appendix prints four methods before and after the official fix with identical stage sub-scores; the fix adds +2.6 to +3.5 combined EPDMS. It also lists LTF at 23.1 in the pre-fix block, so navhard's "lineage B" baselines are pre-fix and lineage A's +2.0 on the same LTF sub-scores is most simply the correction. Calibrated this way, the product-of-stages check separates pre-fix (−1.0 to +2.0) from corrected rows (−1.1 to −4.5) as a tendency, and DriveFuture's leaderboard table mixes them: its own 55.5 and the rows copied from DrivoR are corrected-like, GTRS-E 49.4, DiffVLA 45.0 and DriveSuprim 42.1 pre-fix-like ([[concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md#evaluator-fix]]). Its own rows are corrected-like (−0.96, −0.80) and TransFuser, Hydra-MDP++, DriveSuprim and DiffusionDrive pre-fix-like, so "outperforms all SOTA" (+4.4 over DiffusionDrive as printed) is +0.7 on one scale ([[concepts/navsim-benchmark.md#dvgt2]]). [[sources/clover.md]] prints both columns for its own row (87.2* / 90.4, a +3.2 fix) and for DiffusionDriveV2 (85.5 / 87.5) and SparseDriveV2 (86.7 / 90.1), the same pairs [[sources/wa-jepa.md]] prints three months later; the SparseDriveV2 delta is now reported by two independent groups. *(2026-10-14, withdrawn after ingesting [[sources/sparsedrivev2.md]]: both pairs first appear in SparseDriveV2's own table, two months before CLOVER, so the later prints are copies of one source.)* Every labelled row's residual sign agrees with its column. ### How much of NAVSIM-v2 EPDMS measures frame-to-frame consistency rather than planning?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: On frozen candidate pools scored with simulator sub-scores, v1 and v2 choose different candidates in 43% of scenes, extended comfort accounts for 98% of the utility gap, and choosing for v2 costs 1.3 PDMS on v1 ([[sources/world4scorer.md]]). Selecting with the benchmark's own EC criterion against the previous frame's plan adds about +1.6 EPDMS to two different planners at fixed candidates (World4Scorer 91.4 → 93.0, DrivoR 90.66 → 92.27) and lifts EC above the human agent's. [[sources/drive-jepa.md]]'s momentum-aware selector is the same kind of fix (EC 47.9 → 84.8), and per-frame selection without one scores EC 35–44 ([[sources/ad-e2e-jepa.md]]). Both fixes need an agent that sees navtest frames in log order and remembers its last plan, which standard NAVSIM evaluation does not assume. World4Scorer's changed selections move closer to the human plan (1.87 → 1.51 m), but no paper tests whether the EC gain shows up as smoother closed-loop driving. [[sources/dvgt-2.md]]'s NAVSIM fine-tune raises EPDMS +0.7 while EC falls 83.2 → 77.0 with the same truncated-diffusion head, so training alone moves EC by 6 points; [[sources/geowam.md]], initialized from it with a deterministic head, scores EC 86.8. A third fix, and the first that needs no state: [[sources/clover.md]]'s diverse 64-candidate pool scores EC 35.0 when selected frame by frame, and penalizing distance from a separate single-mode planner's trajectory restores 75.5 (EPDMS 89.3 → 90.4, EP −5.5). DrivoR's narrower pool reaches EC 75.5 with no fix, so the anchor buys back what diversity cost. It runs a second network, and its weights were swept on navtest. A fixed grid pays the per-frame cost too: [[sources/sparsedrivev2.md]] selects from 200 composed trajectories of a 262,144-combination grid and scores EC 78.4, with no continuity term. The other fixed-vocabulary rows in its table sit at 70.9–77.9 (Hydra-MDP++, DriveSuprim), and its single-trajectory and diffusion rows at 87.2–91.0 (Transfuser, DiffusionDriveV2).

### Is NAVSIM-v1 saturated?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: *Partly answered for candidate quality (2026-10-14 lint): three independent oracle measurements reach or pass the human score (Curious-VLA's best-of-6 94.8; World4Scorer's oracle 99.33 over DrivoR's 64-candidate pools; ManeuverFlow's best-of-256 94.85 from a one-step ResNet-34 generator), so good-enough candidates exist and what remains open is whether single-pass gains above about 93 measure selection or driving.* Best-of-6 reaches 94.8 PDMS, the human ground-truth score (Curious-VLA). Single-pass scores now reach 94.0, trained on navtrain ([[sources/world4scorer.md]]) and with the val split added ([[sources/mm-future.md]]), above CLEAR and DA-WAM at 93.7, and almost every entry above 92.0 trains a scorer or an RL reward against the benchmark's own metric. If oracle selection already matches the logged human, gains above about 93 may measure selection quality and not driving quality. World4Scorer's 94.0 comes from a scorer trained on simulator outcomes. On DrivoR's released 64-candidate pools the oracle is 99.33 PDMS, DrivoR's own scorer 93.69 and World4Scorer's 94.19: about 5 points of selection headroom remain over a generated pool. From a modest generator: [[sources/maneuverflow.md]]'s frozen one-step ResNet-34 generator reaches best-of-8 92.20, best-of-64 94.25 and best-of-256 94.85, past the human agent's 94.8, while its own scorer and refiner deliver 90.06–90.58 across 8 to 128 candidates. So human-level oracle scores need only a few hundred candidates from an ordinary planner, and the deployable score is set by selection. Single-pass, [[sources/drivor.md]] reaches 94.6 with navval and 134k SimScale scenes added (93.1 on navtrain alone), 0.2 below the human; its appendix also carries TransDiffuser's best-of-N 94.9, above the human. [[sources/clover.md]] reaches 94.5 single-pass (training split unstated; its baseline is DrivoR's trainval model), 0.3 below the human. Its 64-candidate oracle is 99.33 for DrivoR's pool, 99.76 after coverage training and 99.39 after self-distillation, and by its own decomposition +0.69 of its +0.74 over DrivoR is ranking, +0.05 candidates. **A single-pass model now ties the human**: [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]] scores 94.8 trained on navtrain + navval with no synthetic data (94.85 at 12 denoising steps, the best of a sweep on navtest; 93.6 on navtrain alone). The tie is in PDMS only. The human scores NC, DAC and TTC 100 with EP 87.5; ChainFlow-VLA scores NC 99.2, DAC 99.0, TTC 97.2 with EP 91.9. At the top of the ladder, extra progress buys back rule violations, so "human-level PDMS" no longer means human-like driving. It reports no oracle, no v2 and no navhard. Four DrivoR descendants now occupy 94.0–94.8 ([[concepts/navsim-benchmark.md#chainflow]]). Test-time search confirms it from the other side: [[sources/toad.md]]'s CEM search against DrivoR's scorer adds +0.1 PDMS to RAP-DINO (93.8) and to DrivoR + 134k SimScale (94.6 → 94.7), against +1.7 to +2.1 for weaker planners (iPad 91.7, ZTRS 86.9). The gains are almost entirely ego progress. Another oracle near the human: [[sources/raf-vla.md]]'s best-of-6 is 94.2, from a 90.0 single-sample VLA.

### Do NAVSIM rankings survive a reactive closed loop?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: The only closed-loop reproduction of NAVSIM leaders inverts the order: in AlpaSim, SimWAM (91.5 PDMS) is last on at-fault score (0.30) and Alpamayo-R1, which reports no NAVSIM number, is first (0.58) ([[sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md]]). Of the entries above 92 PDMS, two have a closed-loop number: [[sources/mm-future.md]] (93.4), 32.3 HD-Score on HUGSIM, below WA-JEPA's 44.6 (91.8 PDMS), and World4Scorer on Bench2Drive (below). A fourth closed-loop benchmark: [[sources/maneuverflow.md]] runs WorldEngine, 288 high-risk scenes reconstructed in 3DGS from navtest logs, with logged or IDM traffic. ### How much of a navhard score is the generator and how much is the selector?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: [[sources/drivefuture.md]] is the only paper to report one checkpoint with and without a scorer: 30.9 → 34.6 (its world model) → 55.5 (GTRS-Dense over 100 proposals). Every navhard entry above 42 scores its own candidates and none of the others reports an unscored ablation. [[sources/momworld.md]] puts a predicted future inside a strong scorer and is +1.1 over GTRS-Dense's published score, but that base is not a matched rerun and the page is held at low confidence. Whether future conditioning survives a strong scorer is still open. On navtest there are two partial measurements: [[sources/physwam.md]] reports first sample, a medoid-of-8 selector and the oracle on one checkpoint (90.3 → 90.4 EPDMS for the selector), and [[sources/mm-future.md]] prices a learned scorer at +8.2 PDMS between a one-trajectory and a 32-trajectory model. A third ingredient is now priced: training data. [[sources/simscale.md]] adds 3DGS-rendered off-log scenes with corrective labels and gains +5.1 to +5.8 for two unscored planners and +6.1 to +8.6 for a scorer (GTRS-Dense), so data and selection add rather than substitute. The 53.2 "SimScale" entry in the scorer cohort is therefore a data-plus-scorer pipeline. A navtest scorer swap at fixed candidates now exists: on DrivoR's released pools, [[sources/world4scorer.md]]'s scorer scores 94.19 PDMS against DrivoR's own 93.69 (pool oracle 99.33; no ranking signal 71.10). It reports no navhard, although DrivoR is second on that leaderboard. [[sources/idol.md]] scores its 256 refined anchors with WoTE's reward model (trained on five cached PDM metrics) and reaches 38.0 with a camera-only ResNet-34 variant, the lowest scored entry in the cohort; it reports no unscored or unrefined navhard run, and its margins over its baselines cross evaluator scales. A navtest version of the split, from one paper: with [[sources/maneuverflow.md]]'s generator frozen, its best-of-N rises 92.20 → 94.25 from 8 to 64 candidates while the deployed (scored and refined) plan rises 90.06 → 90.58, and 128 candidates add nothing. Its learned scorer picks the pool's evaluator-best candidate first in 40% of scenes and one within a PDMS point of it in 75%. On this generator the selector is the binding constraint. No navhard. [[sources/drivor.md]] decomposes a navhard leader on one encoder: its ViT-S register encoder under GTRS-Dense's vocabulary scorer 45.8 (GTRS-Dense on V2-99: 45.0), its own generator and disentangled scorer 48.3, and with SimScale's synthetic scenes 52.3 (+65k) and 54.6 (+134k). So of its 9.6-point lead over GTRS-Dense, 0.8 is the encoder, 2.5 the heads and 6.3 training data. The wiki had carried 54.6 as an architecture result. [[sources/clover.md]] retrains DrivoR's generator and scorer against the evaluator: +0.8 PDMS on navtest, +0.0 on navhard (48.3 against DrivoR's 48.3, same corrected scale), with Stage-1 DAC +2.9 and EC −17 ([[concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md#clover]]). **The strongest selector-side evidence yet**: [[sources/toad.md]] puts one scorer, DrivoR's (probably its +134k-SimScale checkpoint), behind six generators as the reward of a test-time search. Their navhard spread shrinks from 34.7–54.6 to 49.0–56.3. For iPad, re-ranking its own proposals with that scorer is 72% of the gain (34.7 → 45.6 → 49.8 with search). For Hydra-MDP, re-ranking hurts (37.3) and search gives 49.7. Searching with GTRS's or SparseDriveV2's scorer leaves every generator at or below its base. Generator quality still orders the results (DrivoR's pool, the best start, ends highest), but most of a weak generator's navhard deficit is recoverable by the scorer.

### Is the navhard Stage-2 lane-keeping collapse a planner failure or a rendering artifact?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: Every method, including constant velocity, loses 35–45 points of lane keeping in Stage 2. If 3DGS renderings degrade as the ego pose leaves the recorded trajectory, part of the drop measures the benchmark. A candidate scorer recovers 10 points of Stage-2 LK on a fixed checkpoint, which fits either explanation. No paper has separated them. The rendering explanation is plausible: in a reprojective simulator, [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]] reports artifacts that grow with the ego's offset from the log (range kept under 4 m) and correlate with it strongly enough for a policy to exploit. That is reprojection, not 3DGS. **Partly answered by a planner that sees no rendering.** PDM-Closed, run on ground-truth state, loses 25.6 points of Stage-2 lane keeping (99.3 → 73.7), with NC, DAC and TTC also down 6–10 points ([[sources/simscale.md]]). At least that much of the drop is in the Stage-2 scenarios. Learned planners trained on real data lose 37–50 points in the same table, so 10–25 points remain unexplained. Co-training on 3DGS renders of off-log states recovers 10–14 of them for unscored planners (LTF 47.9 → 58.3, DiffusionDrive 46.4 → 60.8), which still fits both explanations.

### Does a navhard gain from 3DGS training data measure recovery or renderer familiarity?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/simscale.md]] co-trains planners on 3DGS-rendered off-log states and gains most where the test is also 3DGS-rendered at displaced poses (navhard Stage 2), less in Stage 1 and least on real-image navtest. Rendering quality matters by up to 1.6 EPDMS (PSNR ≥ 27 vs < 27). The training engine and the benchmark may share a codebase. The separating experiment, rendering the unperturbed log through the same engine with the human label, has not been run; nor has a test on a non-3DGS closed-loop benchmark. A fourth planner family shows the same shape: [[sources/drivor.md]] gains +4.0 and +6.3 navhard EPDMS from 65k and 134k SimScale scenes and +0.3 / +0.9 PDMS on navtest (over its trainval model, if the paper's figure is right about the base). If its scorer, used as a search reward for five other planners, is that checkpoint's, then their navhard gains (+6 to +15 EPDMS for the four weakest, against +0.1 to +2.1 PDMS on navtest) partly transfer SimScale-trained judgement to planners never trained on 3DGS renders. The paper does not say which scorer checkpoint it uses.

### Does the expert that generated Bench2Drive's data bound its leaderboard?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Bench2Drive's official training data comes from [[sources/think2drive.md]], a privileged BEV planner trained by model-based RL that scores 91.85 DS / 85.41 SR on the benchmark; the rule-based PDM-Lite, the other expert protocol, scores 97.02 / 92.27 (both from [[sources/dynvla.md]]'s table). The best Think2Drive-protocol student in the wiki is 5.6 DS below its teacher ([[sources/deepsight.md]], 86.23) and LinkVLA, protocol undisclosed, is 0.84 below it. Tighter coupling to the expert does not help: [[sources/raw2drive.md]] trains a camera policy with Think2Drive's own world model, reward heads and policy and lands 20.5 DS below it (71.36), further than imitation students of the expert's data. [[sources/sparsedrivev2.md]] scores 89.15 DS / 70.00 SR by imitation alone, 2.7 DS below Think2Drive, and states neither its training data nor its expert protocol. If it used the base set, it is the closest Think2Drive-protocol student in the wiki. [[sources/lvdrive.md]] states its data, the official base set: 80.71 DS, 11.1 below the expert, with give way at 20 against the expert's 90. With PDM-Lite itself as the takeover expert the student reaches 87.85. With RoG-Expert, a PDM-Lite variant that drives worse (93.41 DS / 81.81 SR, front-field-of-view-blinded, give way 40 against PDM-Lite's 80) but whose labels are rollout-checked and timed near the last solvable state, it reaches **90.34 / 73.51**: 6.7 DS below PDM-Lite (97.02) and 3.1 below its own post-training expert. The trigger also changes between the two runs. So on this side of the protocol split the student is limited less by the expert's driving score than by whether its labels are usable from the student's states ([[concepts/bench2drive.md#rog-dagger]], [[concepts/pdm-lite.md#rog-dagger]]).

## 2. World models: what the predicted future is for

### Does world modeling buy open-loop accuracy or closed-loop robustness?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: Four effects on navtest / navhard point the same way: [[sources/geowam.md]] future geometry +0.6 / +4.9, [[sources/suv.md]] future access +0.3 / +4.1, [[sources/metis.md]] asymmetric mask +0.5 / +2.2 and video-prior scale ≈0 / +2.4. One mechanism breaks the pattern: [[sources/physwam.md]]'s geometric loss is worth +1.9 on both. Most world-model papers optimize and report navtest only, which may be the wrong benchmark for the question. By analogy only: when a world model is used as the training environment instead ([[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]]), held-out trajectory error gets worse (0.361 → 0.394) and closed-loop lane-centre tests go from 5/24 to 24/24. A caution on reading the asymmetry: [[sources/simscale.md]] shows the same navtest-small / navhard-large shape with no world model at all. Co-training on 3DGS-rendered off-log states is worth +0.8 to +2.9 on navtest and +5.1 to +8.6 on navhard for three planner families. So the asymmetry may belong to anything that exposes a planner to off-log states (or to 3DGS images), not to world modeling in particular. The same shape appears off navhard: [[sources/hyworldvla.md]]'s fine-tuning latent loss is worth +0.42 PDMS on navtest and +13.7 on a private 655-scene rain/fog subset of the OpenScene test split. That subset is weakly calibrated (see the new question on weather robustness below). [[sources/idol.md]] is +1.7 PDMS over WoTE on navtest, +10.1 on two-stage navhard and +1.51 DS on Bench2Drive. The navhard gap is probably about half that on a common evaluator scale, and IDOL's mechanism ablations are navtest-only, so it does not separate the two readings. GeoWAM's +0.6 / +4.9 is not a single addition: [[sources/dvgt-2.md]] shows its baseline row is DVGT-2-NAVSIM and its trajectory head truncated diffusion with 20 anchors, which GeoWAM replaces with deterministic regression while adding forecasting. The two changes are priced together. The one paper with a world-model ablation in both an open-loop benchmark and closed-loop CARLA points the other way: [[sources/law.md]]'s latent loss is worth +7.1 PDMS on NAVSIM and −0.10 m / −0.11 points on nuScenes, but +2.2 DS on CARLA Town05 Long, inside its run spread (sd 2.1–2.6). That is CARLA 0.9.10 with a TCP head, not navhard. A closed-loop VLA result where the prediction loss alone is null: [[sources/lvdrive.md]] regresses six future front-view latents inside ORION's VLA on Bench2Drive. On Dev10 (10 routes) the loss alone moves DS 65.25 → 66.31 (SR 4 → 3 of 10), and only a refiner that reads the prediction lifts it to 82.39. Open-loop L2 changes little (0.63 against ORION's 0.68). The full-benchmark effect is not measured against a matched baseline (80.71 against ORION's 77.74, with different training). [[sources/deepsight.md]]'s full-benchmark world-model effect, +26.4 DS (58.16 → 84.52), comes from a model in which nothing explicitly reads the predicted latents.

### Does generating the future at test time ever pay off?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: On navtest, little: SimWAM finds no benefit over isolation, and [[sources/foresight.md]] pays 870 ms for +0.3 PDMS. The largest navtest effect is a geometric future: [[sources/geoworldad.md]]'s latent future-geometry module, shared by 64 proposals, is worth +1.7 PDMS / +2.8 EPDMS, not compute-matched. On navhard, one-way access is worth +4.1 EPDMS ([[sources/suv.md]]) while the bidirectional version is worst ([[sources/metis.md]]). Whether it matters for long-horizon rollout or reactive closed-loop interaction, which NAVSIM cannot measure, is open. WA-JEPA's ablations never separate its training objective from its inference-time scene denoising. A small navtest effect for a *latent* future: [[sources/foredrive.md]] consumes predicted DINOv3 latents and gains +0.4 to +0.6 PDMS over the same training without consumption, for 26 ms; zeroing them at test costs 1.4 and feeding a wrong future costs 2.4. No navhard. With [[sources/da-wam.md]]'s shared JEPA-latent future at −0.5, shared feature-space futures now disagree in sign on navtest; only photometric shared futures are consistently near zero. A third shared feature-space data point, and a cheap one: [[sources/hyworldvla.md]]'s VLM predicts one video-VAE latent through a query token and an MLP, and letting the action expert read it is worth +0.30 PDMS on navtest (single run, no generation step). Shared feature-space futures now stand at +0.4 to +0.6 (ForeDrive), +0.3 (HyWorldVLA) and −0.5 (DA-WAM). Per-candidate latent futures read back into the candidate: [[sources/idol.md]] rolls out a latent BEV future for each of 256 anchors and refines each anchor's query from its own rollout. Feedback is worth +1.3 PDMS (future states) to +2.7 (a learned encoder of consecutive states plus a second rollout–refine pass) over the same model without feedback, at about 57 ms on an RTX 3090. Not compute-matched against the no-feedback base; navtest only; single runs. A shared latent future read per candidate: [[sources/maneuverflow.md]] predicts one action-free BEV forecast at inference and samples it along each of 64 candidate paths for scoring and refinement. It is worth +1.58 PDMS before refinement and +0.55 after (single runs); the ablation also removes the forecast's training loss, so training-time shaping is not separated out. Nothing is decoded. In closed loop, on ten routes: [[sources/lvdrive.md]] predicts six future latents in the same forward pass as the plan, and a two-layer refiner reads them. The refined trajectory scores 82.39 Dev10 DS, the same model's unrefined one 73.22, and a model trained with the future loss but no reader 66.31 (base 65.25). The cost is 2.03 s against 0.93 s per frame on an H20. These are single Dev10 runs. On all 220 routes no refiner-free arm is reported. [[sources/raf-vla.md]] calls itself generation-free, yet its world queries regress future representations and the action expert reads them at inference, for 1 ms. Loss and reading together are worth +1.21 PDMS (SFT), with no arm separating them. Shared feature-space futures that a planner reads now stand at +0.3 (HyWorldVLA), +0.4 to +0.6 (ForeDrive), +1.2 (RAF-VLA, loss and tokens priced together) and −0.5 (DA-WAM).

### What does a transition encoder over candidate-conditioned futures encode?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/idol.md]] feeds pairs of consecutive predicted BEV states, rolled out under each candidate anchor, through a learned MLP it calls an inverse dynamics model. At equal parameters it beats a reader of the states alone by +1.1 PDMS and of fixed latent differences by +0.9 (single runs, navtest). Because each rollout is generated under its own anchor, the action behind the transition is already known, and the module's only direct target is the expert's correction to that anchor (speed, acceleration and heading-rate differences). The wiki's earlier inverse-dynamics planners read an action from a future generated without it ([[sources/drivewam.md]], [[sources/futuresightdrive.md]]). No probe of the module's features and no ablation of its correction loss is reported, so "inverse dynamics" and "learned residual correction over imagined pairs" are not separated.

### Are per-candidate futures really futures, and do they help at a realistic horizon?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Two per-candidate-future scorers agree in sign and size: +0.15 PDMS ([[sources/da-wam.md]], 0.5 s futures) and +0.4 for letting the scorer read the future ([[sources/mm-future.md]], 4 s futures). In both, only the expert-matched future is supervised (31 of 32 and 63 of 64 per scene are not). MM-Future shows action sensitivity (futures of extreme trajectories are 17% further apart in RMS), not correctness. [[sources/ad-e2e-jepa.md]] offers a hit-rate instrument (53.8% top-1 among 256) and shows 4 s rollouts cost about 3 ms per candidate, but neither DA-WAM nor MM-Future has been measured with it. A third design avoids unsupervised futures by not predicting appearance for alternatives: [[sources/world4scorer.md]] supervises every candidate's latent state with simulator outcomes and only the logged trajectory's with a +2 s feature. Its tests do not establish that the states are futures: shuffling them breaks selection, but just as much for DrivoR's non-predictive scorer, and they cluster by outcome only slightly more than raw trajectories do (80.2% vs 76.8%, on training scenes). A third use of them: [[sources/idol.md]] refines each candidate from its own rollout before scoring, rather than scoring the candidate against its future, and the refinement is worth more on navtest (+2.7) than either scorer design (+0.15, +0.4). Whether the non-expert rollouts are supervised is not stated, the rollout length is not given, and the per-candidate states are never checked against any future. A fourth configuration, shared prediction with per-candidate readout: [[sources/maneuverflow.md]] predicts one 1–4 s BEV forecast and samples it at each candidate's footprint, +1.58 PDMS before refinement (against +0.15 and +0.4 for DA-WAM's and MM-Future's per-candidate futures, and −0.50 for DA-WAM's shared one). Its forecast is action-free, so its per-candidate evidence is spatial, not counterfactual: it shows what is predicted where each path goes, not how agents would respond to it. See the new question on readout below.

### Do the two world-model supervision rules compose?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Objective form follows target entropy: regression on multi-view scene latents is worse than no future prediction (90.7 vs 91.1 EPDMS, [[sources/wa-jepa.md]]). Target content follows horizon: optical flow is best far out and RGB one step ahead ([[sources/drive-hwm.md]]). Nobody has crossed them. Two results complicate the first rule: L1 regression on future latents helps in [[sources/redrive.md]] (+0.7), and [[sources/geowam.md]] pairs cosine alignment with dense point-map regression without collapsing. It is also unknown which other deterministic predictors (DeepSight, FLARE, Latent-WAM, AD-E2E-JEPA) are losing performance to the same effect. A third data point on the harmful side: [[sources/resworld.md]]'s residual-input world model, trained only by the planning loss, gets worse when its predicted map is supervised toward the real next-frame map (nuScenes collision 0.17% → 0.21%, L2 0.59 → 0.61 m). The loss form is unstated and the difference is a few samples. A regression case on the helpful side: [[sources/foredrive.md]] regresses (L1) single-front-camera DINOv3 latents, and consuming the regressed futures helps (+0.4 to +0.6), consistent with the entropy reading (one camera, a lower-entropy target than WA-JEPA's four). Its predictions are over-smoothed and the planner ignores their order. Another low-entropy regression target on the helpful side: [[sources/world4scorer.md]]'s cosine loss on one pooled 384-d front-view feature 2 s ahead (effective rank 49) is worth +0.95 PDMS in one run. A two-sided case: [[sources/hyworldvla.md]] regresses (MSE) a single-camera video-VAE latent, a pixel-reconstruction code. On navtest it helps at weight 0.1 (+0.42 over none in fine-tuning) and hurts at 0.2 and 1.0 (90.01, 89.75 against 90.59); on its rain/fog subset it helps monotonically up to 1.0 (73.18 → 92.94). A horizon sweep on the helpful side: [[sources/law.md]] regresses (MSE) six per-camera latent vectors and finds it helps at 0.5 s (0.61 m / 0.30% against 0.71 / 0.41 without), most at 1.5 s (0.58 / 0.25), less at 3 s (0.63 / 0.27), and hurts at 10 s (0.72 / 0.43), consistent with regression degrading as the target grows more uncertain. The 10 s run may also see less data (targets near scene ends), which the paper does not address. A target-dimension window under one closed-loop VLA: [[sources/lvdrive.md]] regresses (cosine + L1) future front-view latents from four frozen extractors with the same refiner. On Dev10: VQGAN-ImageNet codes (256-d, quantized) 82.39 DS, DINOv3-L (1024-d) 71.72, its own encoder 65.42, Emu3's MoVQGAN codes (4-d) 59.91, against 65.25 with no target. A low-entropy quantized target wins, a wide continuous one helps less and a 4-d one hurts. That fits the entropy reading with a capacity floor added, but each cell is one run on ten routes. MSE regression helps in [[sources/raf-vla.md]] too (+1.21 PDMS in SFT), from four query states per horizon onto a frozen Cosmos encoder's representation (target shape unstated). 1 s and 4 s each give about +1.0 and together +1.21; adding 2 s, or 2 s and 3 s, falls back to +0.7 (single navtest runs).

### How much of a "world-model-shaped" representation is world modeling?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/redrive.md]]'s encoder keeps its gain under a frozen probe (86.7 → 90.2), but it was fine-tuned by both the planning loss and the future loss, and the paper's own split is +3.5 for unfreezing against +0.7 for future prediction. The same question applies to every training-time-only world model that reports a with/without-future-loss delta only end to end. Related: [[sources/physwam.md]] does not isolate whether its +1.9 EPDMS needs the depth–motion cross term or only a pose loss. [[sources/resworld.md]] is the limiting case: its "world model" has no future loss at all, adding one hurts, and its first-pass trajectory, which has the baseline's architecture, keeps most of the gain (nuScenes 0.65 → 0.61 m, 0.28 → 0.18% collision, against 0.59 / 0.17 for the full model). The shaping is done by the planning loss routed through a future-named branch. [[sources/foredrive.md]] separates forecast quality from usefulness: forecasting as an auxiliary loss is worth +0.1; across four training paradigms, the most accurate forecasts (latent cosine 0.843) plan worst (87.9) and the least accurate (0.759) best (89.9); a frozen probe reads the expert trajectory better from its predicted latents (ADE 1.55) than from the real future latents (2.10); shuffling horizons at test changes nothing, and real future latents add only +0.2. Its "future" works as planning-shaped context. [[sources/world4scorer.md]] runs the control this question needs, once: the same auxiliary objective and pathway with a current-frame target scores 92.70 PDMS, with a +2 s target 93.96, with none 93.01. Single seed, last epoch, and the two targets have mean cosine 0.94. [[sources/simvta.md]] adds two content controls during RL. Its video loss is worth +2.2 PDMS only when its gradients reach the encoder (+0.0 into the video head alone), and shuffling the targets among a scene's trajectories puts it 6.0 below no video at all, with NC, DAC and TTC down 3–5 points. So the content of the target matters, in the harmful direction most of all. The shuffle also breaks the frame–pose pairing in the encoder's own history input, which may account for part of the collapse, and the logged-future control is missing (see the new question in section 2). [[sources/worldrft.md]] is a planning-oriented latent world model whose ablations never remove the world-model loss: every row keeps LAW's next-frame latent MSE, and the measured gains are a frozen VGGT (+1.3 PDMS), three planning subtasks with refinement (+1.5) and collision-reward RL (+0.8). The trajectory input is not detached and the target is the logged next frame, so the action-conditioned gain may partly be a second imitation loss routed through the world model; no detached-trajectory arm exists. A target-content control in closed loop: [[sources/lvdrive.md]] keeps its refiner and placeholder tokens fixed and swaps the frozen extractor that supplies future targets. Dev10 DS ranges from 59.91 to 82.39, and its own vision encoder as target scores the same as no target (65.42 vs 65.25). The extra tokens and reader do nothing without an informative target. Single runs on ten routes; no current-frame target. [[sources/raf-vla.md]] adds eight world queries to a VLA and regresses them onto a frozen Cosmos encoder's future-frame representation: +1.21 PDMS after SFT, +1.18 after GRPO. Its baseline drops the queries along with the loss, and it has no current-frame or shuffled target, so the gain is not shown to need a future rather than extra readable tokens with any visual target. A limiting case on the generative side: [[sources/drivewla.md]]'s "World Expert" (a video DiT) receives no video loss at all during training. Its future positions start from noise and are shaped only through attention by the trajectory loss, yet removing it costs 4.6 PDMS. Its PCA figure shows the two future latents (first and second 2 s) nearly identical. The gain belongs to a video-pretrained encoder fine-tuned for planning; whether it predicts is untested.

### Does a world-model auxiliary gain made in SFT survive RL fine-tuning?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: Once, yes. [[sources/raf-vla.md]] runs a 2×2: future-representation alignment during SFT is worth +1.21 PDMS (87.92 → 89.13), and after GRPO on PDMS, with the alignment loss switched off and the world queries frozen, +1.18 (88.85 → 90.03). RL adds +0.93 and +0.90 in the two arms, so the two effects add. With alignment, RL also stops costing NC and TTC. Keeping the auxiliary on during RL is measured once, in [[sources/simvta.md]]: a video loss whose gradients reach the encoder adds +2.2 over RL without video. No paper compares dropping the auxiliary in RL with keeping it, in one model. [[sources/flare.md]] (future DINOv2 features, then GRPO; 86.9 → 91.4) and [[sources/worldrft.md]] (LAW's latent loss, then GRPO; +0.8) report no RL arm without their auxiliary. All single runs on navtest; whether the surviving gain shows up on navhard or in closed loop is untested.
- **See**: [[sources/raf-vla.md]], [[sources/simvta.md]], [[sources/flare.md]], [[sources/worldrft.md]], [[concepts/rl-for-ad.md#raf-vla]], [[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md#future-aligned-world-queries]]
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19

### Does RL post-training of a world-action model need video targets for its own trajectories, or only a retained video loss?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/simvta.md]] keeps Epona's video loss running during trajectory GRPO and supplies its targets by rendering 3DGS futures along the policy's own sampled, executed paths. On navtest: RL without video 89.1 PDMS; video loss into the video head only 89.1; targets rendered once from the initial policy and cached 90.4; targets rendered each round 91.3; the same targets shuffled among a scene's trajectories 83.1 (three seeds for the first and fourth, sd at most 0.1). Matched off-log targets therefore beat none and beat mismatched ones. The arm that would separate "render the policy's futures" from "keep any video loss during RL", GRPO with the logged-trajectory, logged-video loss from pretraining, is not run, and no video-loss arm is reported on navhard or in closed loop. The pretraining-time version of the effect is larger: Epona loses 8.1 PDMS without joint video training ([[sources/epona.md]]).

- **Updated**: 2026-10-14

### Can a world model replace the simulator as the source of reward or cost?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: [[sources/dreameraD.md]] shows a latent reward model can replace simulator calls during RL rollout (87.7 EPDMS) but still needs the simulator to annotate its vocabulary. [[sources/ad-e2e-jepa.md]] has cheap 4 s rollouts over 256–8,192 candidates but selects with the ground-truth future frame; that oracle-goal search reaches only 67.3–72.9 EPDMS against 85.4 for the same paper's imitation model. Nobody has attached a learned reward or goal predictor to a compressed rollout model and reported a real planning score. For supervision other than reward, the answer is yes: [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]] replaces the simulator entirely, as frame generator and as source of action labels (a future-anchored plan head), and the resulting steering policy is deployed in openpilot. Lateral control only. The earliest instance in the wiki is in CARLA: [[sources/think2drive.md]] trains a privileged BEV planner entirely on a DreamerV3 world model's predicted rewards and terminations (83.8 DS on 390 single-scenario routes, against 57.5 for model-free PPO with the same engineering). As in DreamerAD, the reward head is learned from the simulator's reward, and unlike DreamerAD the simulator keeps supplying the current policy's experience. Where the reward is learned matters: [[sources/raw2drive.md]] finds a reward head on camera latents destabilizes training (10-clip Dev10 DS 83.5 → 46.6, adding a continuation head 34.5), because near-identical frames carry opposite rewards, and takes the reward from a world model of ground-truth BEV instead. DreamerAD's dense multi-horizon reward on video latents is the opposite result under different targets.
- **See**: [[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md]], [[concepts/data-driven-simulators.md]], [[sources/think2drive.md]], [[sources/raw2drive.md]]
- **Updated**: 2026-10-06

### Can a driving world model predict counterfactuals?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: For retrospective counterfactuals over recorded episodes, no: action-conditioned generation recovers 0.38 (Vista) and 0.31 (DrivingWorld), below the 0.5 no-preference point ([[sources/driving-wm-counterfactuals.md]]). Open parts: whether a model trained to condition on the factual continuation (abduction) would beat the paper's depth-plus-splatting transport, whether the failure is causal or a domain shift to CARLA renders, whether anything carries over to decision time, and whether any counterfactual metric predicts planning quality. A model conditioned on the history and the recorded continuation now exists ([[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]]'s future-anchored world model), but it uses the continuation as an endpoint the rollout must reach and has not been evaluated on counterfactuals.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-02

### Is a single-pass video backbone, trained only by the trajectory loss, predicting the future?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Several planners read a video generator in one forward pass instead of generating, and some train it only through the planning loss. [[sources/drivewla.md]]'s DiT runs over observed frames plus Gaussian-noise future positions, receives no video loss, and is fine-tuned by the trajectory loss through attention. It is worth +4.6 PDMS (+2.8 for fine-tuning over freezing), and its PCA figure shows the two future latents (first and second 2 s) nearly identical. [[sources/drivelaw.md]] reads a one-step latent and collapses when iterated to ten steps (89.1 → 23.2). [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]] finds readout depth matters about 40 times more than noise level. [[sources/simwam.md]] uses its video backbone only in training. In each case the planner benefits from the video-pretrained network. But no paper probes the future positions against logged future latents, shuffles the observed frames at test time, or compares with a matched video-pretrained encoder without future positions, so a world-model reading and a pretrained-encoder reading predict every reported number equally well.
- **See**: [[sources/drivewla.md]], [[sources/drivelaw.md]], [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]], [[sources/simwam.md]], [[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md#single-pass-world-expert]]
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19

### Where and how should a planner read a video prior?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]] shows readout depth matters about 40 times more than noise level (4.80 vs 0.15 PDMS) and that a mid-network exit beats the final block, on one backbone family with one head. Three papers agree a single forward pass suffices and ForeSight disagrees (100 steps). DriveLaW's collapse at ten denoising iterations (89.1 → 23.2) is undiagnosed. No other paper reports which layer it reads. As a scorer backbone on fixed 64-candidate pools, Wan2.2-5B block-15 features select 92.8 navtest PDMS against 92.4 for DINOv2-S, with the largest train–test gap of five backbones (97.7 train) ([[sources/clover.md]]). Another single-pass reader: [[sources/drivewla.md]] runs DriveLaW-Video (LTX-2B) once over four observed frames plus Gaussian-noise future positions, and exposes every layer to the action stream through attention. Fine-tuning the DiT on the trajectory loss is worth +2.8 over freezing it (88.0 → 90.8), and removing it −4.6. No layer-wise or noise-level sweep is reported. See the new question below on whether such readers predict anything.
- **See**: [[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md]], [[sources/clover.md]], [[sources/drivewla.md]]
- **Updated**: 2026-10-19

### Does the scale of the video prior matter?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: On navtest, barely: SimWAM holds the planner fixed and gets 90.2 from Wan2.1-1.3B against 90.3 from Wan2.2-5B, while a weak prior (LTX-Video, 88.7) costs and a driving-pretrained one (Cosmos-Predict2.5, 90.4) helps most. On navhard, [[sources/metis.md]] finds the same 1.3B-versus-5B swap worth 2.4 EPDMS. Scale may matter exactly where navtest cannot see it. Zero-shot transfer, DriveVA's distinguishing claim, is untested. A cross-paper hint against scale: [[sources/drivewla.md]] reaches 92.3 PDMS with LTX-Video-2B, above SimWAM (91.5), DriveWAM (90.1) and BrainWAM (89.5) on Wan2.2-5B. The designs differ in almost every other respect (a VLM expert, RL, readout), so this is not a scale test.

### What happens when a planner that consumes a predicted future is given a wrong one?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/foredrive.md]] measures it on a fixed checkpoint: removing the predicted future costs 1.4 PDMS (mostly progress and drivable area), while a structured but wrong future (another scene's prediction, or the current latent copied forward) costs 2.4, with TTC down 2 points. Two other papers found the mirror problem on the training side: a planner conditioned on a clean or oracle future it cannot produce at inference degrades ([[sources/drivelaw.md]] 89.1 → 23.2; [[sources/drivefuture.md]] 28.9 when the oracle is held too long). No paper measures how often a deployed world model's prediction is wrong in the way that hurts, or tests a planner that can detect an unreliable future.
- **See**: [[sources/foredrive.md]], [[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md]]
- **Updated**: 2026-10-06

### Does training a planner on its own multi-step latent rollouts make it robust to its own deviations?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: No ingested paper runs the full loop on NAVSIM: plan, predict the next latent under that plan, re-plan from the predicted latent, and supervise the plans made from those self-generated states. The loop exists only with RL in CARLA: [[sources/think2drive.md]] trains an actor-critic on 15-step imagined rollouts of a privileged-BEV world model that is refit on the policy's own experience (83.8 vs 57.5 DS for PPO with the same engineering), and [[sources/raw2drive.md]] carries it to cameras by aligning a camera encoder into the privileged latent space (71.36 DS on Bench2Drive). With labels and no reward, [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]] trains a steering policy on its own rollouts in a 500M world model, each reached state labelled by a plan model that sees 1 s of the recorded future: lane-centre tests 24/24 against 5/24 off-policy, while held-out open-loop error worsens (0.361 → 0.394). It is lateral only, with a separate world model and policy, and not on NAVSIM. On NAVSIM the pieces appear separately. [[sources/dreameraD.md]] rolls each sampled trajectory through a latent world model learned from fixed logs and scores it with a learned reward (88.7 PDMS / 87.7 EPDMS); the policy does not re-plan from the imagined latents. [[sources/idol.md]] reads per-candidate latent rollouts back into the candidates and rolls out again, at the same decision time (87.3 → 89.2 → 90.0 PDMS; further iterations 89.4; 38.0 navhard, camera-only). [[sources/redrive.md]]'s Stage 3 conditions a frozen predictor on the planner's own single trajectory (+0.3, bundled with two other losses). Epona's chain-of-forward training feeds the generator its own frames but not the trajectory head, and DriveWAM decodes actions from the clean future latent in training and the generated one at inference ([[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md]]). Why it may matter: planners that read a future degrade when it differs from the one they trained on ([[sources/drivelaw.md]] 89.1 → 23.2 on clean futures; [[sources/drivefuture.md]] 28.9 with the oracle held too long; [[sources/foredrive.md]] −2.4 for a wrong future against −1.4 for none), and off-log training states without any world model are worth +5.1 to +8.6 navhard EPDMS against +0.8 to +2.9 on navtest ([[sources/simscale.md]]). Two obstacles recur in these papers: the label at an imagined off-log state (a reward, a future-anchored relabeller, or log-retrieval / PDM-Closed pseudo-experts), and a world model learned from fixed logs that must extrapolate to exactly the states it is meant to supply (Think2Drive refits on the policy's experience; DreamerAD does not). The game-engine baseline for that loop is now ingested: [[sources/rog-dagger.md]] lets a camera policy drive in CARLA and labels its own states with a privileged expert (DAgger, one round). Its training data had about 0.1% of near-interaction frames below 0.1 s time to collision, against about 17.5% at inference. The gain is largest where deviations compound longest: Longest6 v2 22 → 44 DS, against +3.75 on Bench2Drive. No latent world model is involved; the simulator supplies both the next state and the expert's ground truth.
- **See**: [[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md]], [[concepts/data-driven-simulators.md]], [[concepts/rl-for-ad.md]], [[sources/think2drive.md]], [[sources/raw2drive.md]], [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]], [[sources/dreameraD.md]], [[sources/idol.md]], [[sources/redrive.md]], [[sources/simscale.md]], [[sources/rog-dagger.md]]
- **Updated**: 2026-10-28

### Does generation quality matter for planning?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/reworld.md]] improves nuScenes FVD 81.3 → 61.9 and NAVSIM PDMS 89.1 → 90.4 in one paper through disjoint mechanisms, and never measures whether the video-side objective helps planning. FVD itself is hard to rank on: [[sources/physwam.md]] measures a recorded-versus-recorded FVD floor of 91.5 at 600 clips. The question has a training-environment version: [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]] reports LPIPS improving with world-model size and data (in a figure the clipping lacks; VAE floor 0.148) but trains policies in one world model only, so simulator quality is never linked to policy quality. Within one 3DGS renderer, a little: [[sources/simscale.md]] trains on matched scenes from reconstructions with novel-view PSNR ≥ 27 and < 27, and the high-fidelity data wins all eight cells by +0.1 to +1.6 navhard EPDMS. For latent forecasts the relation inverts within one paper: [[sources/foredrive.md]]'s better-aligned forecasts come with lower PDMS (87.9 at cosine 0.843 vs 89.9 at 0.759). [[sources/simvta.md]] moves video quality and planning together with one intervention: its rendered-target video loss takes FID 20.4 → 13.1, FVD 105.4 → 46.3 and PDMS 89.1 → 91.3 against RL without video. But the no-video arm's video head reads an encoder RL has changed, the initialization's own FID/FVD is not reported, and the arm whose video loss does not reach the encoder (89.1 PDMS) has no video numbers. Video quality is measured only along logged trajectories.
- **See**: [[concepts/world-model-for-ad.md]], [[concepts/navsim-benchmark.md]], [[concepts/data-driven-simulators.md]], [[sources/simscale.md]], [[sources/foredrive.md]], [[sources/simvta.md]]

### Should a world model be given only what moves?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Three papers put an explicit motion quantity into a driving world model. As input, [[sources/resworld.md]] feeds only frame-to-frame differences of ego-aligned scene queries and beats a full-scene input by 0.02 m L2 and 0.04 collision points on nuScenes (single runs, a few samples apart); static structure is copied from the current map instead of predicted. As a target, [[sources/drive-hwm.md]] finds optical flow best at long horizons and RGB best one step ahead. As a state, [[sources/momworld.md]] initializes momentum from the same kind of feature difference but reports no prediction error with or without it. The known cost: stationary agents (parked cars, waiting pedestrians) give no residual and reach the planner only through the static branch, as ResWorld states. No paper has tested a motion-only design on a closed-loop or navhard split.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-02

### Which data-driven simulator trains the best policy: reprojection, reconstruction or a learned world model?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: One paper compares two families under one supervision source. [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]] trains the same steering policy in a reprojective simulator and in a 500M world model: 24/24 against 24/24 on lane-centre tests, 20/20 against 19/20 on lane changes, 0.369 against 0.394 held-out error. The world-model policy is engaged more in the field (29.92% against 27.63% of time), but the two cohorts are not matched. The test is lateral only, while reprojection's stated weakness is longitudinal range (under 4 m). Reconstruction-based training ([[sources/senna2.md]], 3DGS) and latent world-model RL ([[sources/dreameraD.md]]) are each evaluated on different benchmarks with different supervision. [[sources/simscale.md]] adds a reconstruction-based training result on NAVSIM (3DGS with IDM agents; +5 to +9 navhard EPDMS) and compares supervision sources inside it, but no second family. It also differs from openpilot on a second axis: its off-log states come from a fixed perturbation sampler, not from the policy. Reconstruction now has an online-RL result: [[sources/rad.md]] trains PPO in 3,968 3DGS clips and cuts collisions 0.229 → 0.089 against its own imitation-only post-training, on a private benchmark in the same renderer, with log-replayed agents. A reconstruction simulator in a fourth role: [[sources/simvta.md]] renders 3DGS futures along the current policy's executed paths as video-loss targets for a world-action model, while the policy sees only logged images and the reward comes from the PDM scorer. It also gives the wiki's first fixed-versus-on-policy comparison in one renderer: targets cached once from the initial policy 90.4 PDMS, refreshed each round 91.3, none 89.1.

### Does a clean VLM stream suppress an iteratively refined stream in shared attention?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/brainwam.md]] measures it for VLM plus video denoising (Tri-MoT 87.8 below WAM-only 88.1); SimWAM's two-modality ablation shows no such effect without a VLM. Untested for occupancy diffusion, flow-matched latents or JEPA predictors, and untested whether one-way masking (UniDriveVLA, AutoMoT) avoids it. A counter-case where the video stream is not iterated: [[sources/drivewla.md]] puts a frozen VLM (through an adapter), a video DiT read in one pass from noisy future positions, and a flow-matching action DiT in one shared attention. Adding the VLM helps (+1.7, 89.1 → 90.8), and bidirectional VLM↔video exchange beats none by +2.4. Its action stream is iterated (10 Euler steps) and reads the VLM without visible harm. This is consistent with iterative refinement of the video stream being what BrainWAM's Tri-MoT suffers from, but no paper runs both settings.

## 3. Perception and representation

### Is explicit perception supervision necessary for planning?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Both routes work. [[sources/auto-jepa.md]] reaches 91.3 PDMS with a frozen encoder and no perception labels; [[sources/wcog-vla.md]] reaches 92.9 with 3D boxes and per-agent futures. Removing WCog-VLA's 3D perception costs 3.3 PDMS (89.3 → 86.0), yet its 3D pretraining is worth only +1.1 while the planner still emits text, which suggests explicit structure needs a continuous action head to be used. [[sources/foresight.md]] loses 1.1 PDMS (89.3 → 88.2) when its whole perception branch is deleted. No paper compares the supervised and unsupervised routes at matched capacity and data. [[sources/resworld.md]] runs both routes in one NAVSIM codebase: 87.3 PDMS without perception labels, 88.3 with detection and map supervision. The supervised variant also feeds its world model detection agent queries instead of temporal residuals, so the +1.0 is not a clean price. In a model-based RL setting it was: [[sources/raw2drive.md]]'s camera world model scores 17.4 DS on its 10-clip set without a BEV-mask decoder head and 83.5 with it, and aligning its encoder to ground-truth BEV features cell by cell is required (0.0 without any alignment). [[sources/drivor.md]] reaches 93.1 PDMS on navtrain (93.7 trainval) with no perception labels at all, from 64 register tokens of a LoRA-tuned DINOv2-S, which puts the unsupervised route at the top of the v1 ladder among models trained on the standard split. A third route, dense geometry pseudo-labels: [[sources/dvgt-2.md]] (one 1.8B network for point maps, ego pose and trajectory, MoGe-2 depth labels, no boxes or maps) reaches 88.6 PDMS without a NAVSIM-specific stage and 90.3 with one, but never ablates the geometry objective, so it does not price the route. [[sources/geoworldad.md]]'s geometry-pretrained encoder against scratch (89.3 against 84.2) remains the controlled evidence. "Annotation-free" can stop at pretraining: [[sources/worldrft.md]] pretrains on Grounded-SAM pseudo-labels and frozen VGGT features, then fine-tunes with an RL reward computed from annotated 3D boxes of every agent in future frames ([[concepts/teacher-pseudo-labels.md#privileged-teachers]]). The earliest matched comparison: [[sources/law.md]] adds the same latent world-model loss to a perception-free planner and to VAD on nuScenes. Labels still win (0.49 m / 0.19% against 0.61 / 0.30), and the loss helps both. On NAVSIM, where only the perception-free model runs, the loss takes it from 77.5 to 84.6 PDMS, level with camera-only LTF (83.8) and TransFuser (84.0) ([[concepts/perception-for-planning.md#law]]).
- **See**: [[concepts/perception-for-planning.md]], [[sources/raw2drive.md]], [[sources/drivor.md]], [[sources/dvgt-2.md]], [[concepts/perception-for-planning.md#vga]], [[sources/worldrft.md]], [[sources/law.md]], [[concepts/perception-for-planning.md#law]]

### Do teacher pseudo-labels cap what the student can learn?
- **Status**: partially answered
- **Known so far**: Swapping a teacher's depth for LiDAR depth under one planner has not been run. [[sources/physwam.md]] does half of it: generating depth alone adds nothing to planning (87.2 vs 87.5 PDMS without it), while a raw-LiDAR point loss that also involves the generated motion improves depth by 8–15% AbsRel and planning by +1.9 EPDMS. Whether teacher quality (for example Depth Anything V2 vs V3, SAM 2 vs SAM 3) propagates to planning is untested, and agent-generated reasoning corpora rarely carry a measured noise rate (Qwen-Drive reports 55.9% for its own). For a planning teacher: [[sources/simscale.md]]'s PDM-Closed teacher scores 51.3 on navhard and its students reach 48.0 (single) and 50.9 (three-model ensemble), so the cap is not reached. Benchmark sub-score labels, which no trajectory teacher limits, do slightly better than the teacher's trajectories (+0.3 / +0.8). The teacher's style is inherited: its low extended comfort (EC 36.0 / 25.4) shows up in the students' Stage-2 EC (LTF 70.6 → 42.9, DiffusionDrive 72.8 → 31.9). For a privileged driving teacher: Bench2Drive's base set was generated by [[sources/think2drive.md]], which scores 91.85 DS there; the best Think2Drive-protocol student in the wiki scores 86.23 ([[sources/deepsight.md]]). On the teacher's own benchmark a camera student (TCP, 200K frames) keeps 36.4 of 83.8 DS. See the new question on whether the expert bounds Bench2Drive. A student coupled to the same teacher at every level of training (latent targets, dynamics, rewards, policy initialization and distillation) does worse than imitation students: [[sources/raw2drive.md]] scores 71.36 against the teacher's 91.85. A geometry chain with a monocular teacher at its root: MoGe-2 labels [[sources/dvgt-2.md]], which initializes [[sources/geowam.md]]. DVGT-2's ray depth beats every zero-shot geometry model on five datasets, but it is trained on those datasets and the evaluation ground truth is not stated, so agreement with its own teacher cannot be ruled out. **A case where the teacher's own score is the wrong measure of the teacher.** In [[sources/rog-dagger.md]]'s CARLA DAgger, PDM-Lite (97.02 DS alone) yields a student at 87.85 and a weaker, field-of-view-blinded variant (93.41 alone) one at 90.34, from a base of 86.59. Re-executed from the student's own states, PDM-Lite's labels collide in 29.6% of the 2 s before a takeover, against 0.47% after it. The two runs also differ in takeover trigger. The cap that matters here is the label's feasibility, timing and observability from the student's state, not the teacher's driving ([[concepts/teacher-pseudo-labels.md#teacher-score]]).

### How many visual tokens does a planner need, and from which cameras?

### Is it video pretraining or the JEPA objective that transfers to planning?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: V-JEPA 2 beats image-pretrained encoders in every sweep that includes it ([[sources/redrive.md]]: +5.5 PDMS over DINOv2, and fine-tuning DINOv2 under a predictive loss does not close the gap, 84.4 vs 89.9). But V-JEPA 2 is the only video-pretrained entry in those tables, and no paper includes a video-pretrained non-JEPA control. Video-generation priors (SimWAM, DriveLaW) are also effective without a JEPA objective. [[sources/ad-e2e-jepa.md]] inherits the opposite default (DINOv3 over V-JEPA 2) from a robotics ablation the wiki can cite only second-hand. An image-pretrained encoder under a JEPA-style predictive loss reaches 90.4 PDMS in [[sources/foredrive.md]] (DINOv3 ViT-L, one camera), not far below [[sources/redrive.md]]'s V-JEPA 2 at 91.0. Different planners, so it is not the missing control, but the image-encoder penalty is smaller there than in ReDrive's own DINOv2 run (84.4). *(Corrected at the 2026-10-15 lint: "no paper includes a video-pretrained non-JEPA control" no longer holds.)* [[sources/drive-hwm.md]] swaps three video-pretrained backbones into its future-predictor slot with everything else fixed: V-JEPA 93.8, WAN 93.2, CogVideo 93.0 PDMS. So the joint-embedding objective beats two generative video objectives by 0.6–0.8 once video pretraining is held constant. It is the predictor slot, not the planner's encoder; the arms are not capacity-matched; single runs; and Drive-HWM's own scores are disputed (93.8 in tables, 93.3 in text) ([[concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md#jepa-vs-generative]]).

### When should a foundation backbone be frozen, LoRA-adapted or fully fine-tuned?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Off-the-shelf use is priced three times: a BEV head on frozen Qwen-initialized features trails a dedicated detector by 6.34 mAP and unfreezing recovers +10.46; a frozen video DiT costs 6.42 PDMS; LoRA collapses under DINOv2 geometric distillation. [[sources/redrive.md]] prices unfreezing its encoder at +3.5 PDMS. The page's rule of thumb (LoRA when the pretrained objective already matches the downstream one, full fine-tuning otherwise) is fitted to two papers, neither of which tests the other's setting. [[sources/drivor.md]] gives a clean three-way comparison on one planner (DINOv2-S with registers, navval): frozen 84.4, full fine-tuning 88.4, LoRA 90.0; without compression, frozen 88.2 against LoRA 90.2. The paper attributes the full fine-tuning gap to learning-rate tuning it did not do. A fourth arrangement: [[sources/worldrft.md]] keeps an off-the-shelf VGGT frozen and fuses its final-layer tokens into a trained ResNet encoder with one cross-attention layer, worth +1.3 PDMS at an unreported inference cost.

### Can one driving model plan across datasets and camera rigs without per-dataset fine-tuning?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/dvgt-2.md]] trains one 1.8B model on five datasets with 2–8 cameras and runs it on NAVSIM's 8-camera rig (88.6 PDMS, 88.9 EPDMS) and nuScenes's 6-camera rig (0.78 m / 0.19%, ST-P3 convention) with no dataset-specific stage; a NAVSIM fine-tune still adds +1.7 PDMS. Its 1-second L2 tracks each dataset's share of training batches: 0.20 m on OpenScene (77%), 0.56 m on nuScenes and 0.78 m on Waymo (6% each), 2.12 m on KITTI and 2.00 m on DDAD (5–6%), which the paper attributes to higher speeds and anchors clustered on OpenScene. The NAVSIM-trained world-action models evaluated on nuScenes without fine-tuning use one front camera ([[concepts/nuscenes-waymo-evals.md]]). No paper reports planning on a dataset or rig held out of training. Held-out datasets, open loop: [[sources/drivewla.md]]'s NAVSIM-trained, front-camera model scores 0.94 m / 0.09% on the nuScenes validation split (UniAD definitions, with ego state) and 1.61 m / 1.06% on Bench2Drive's recorded frames, with no training on either. Its collision rate is below the fine-tuned rows in its table, and its L2 above two of them ([[concepts/nuscenes-waymo-evals.md#drivewla]]).

## 4. Reasoning and the VLM

### Does grounded reasoning help on a public benchmark, and is the trace faithful?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/grava.md]] gives the most graded ablation: action-only 74.8 → coarse reasoning 77.9 → box-grounded objects 85.5 → full grounded reasoning 90.1, all RL-trained, on an internal long-tail benchmark. Without RL, full reasoning scores 73.1. Editing the trace edits the action (0.44 → 0.82 normalized PDMS), but the trace's final maneuver is copied from the expert trajectory at annotation time. Unknown: whether the ladder holds on a public benchmark, whether any trace stays predictive of the action on navhard, and whether a verbalized action is enough for auditing.

### What does driving adaptation cost the VLM, and is retained general capability worth anything for driving?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Under one 15-benchmark protocol, Qwen-Drive scores 66.41 against its base model's 67.40 while Alpamayo-1.5 scores 14.65, MiMo-Embodied 26.53 and UniDriveVLA 48.73 ([[sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md]]). The distinguishing variable looks like the data mixture (26% general-purpose); nothing between 0% and 26% has been run. Qwen-Drive freezes the VLM during RL, so retention after GRPO through the VLM is unmeasured for every method that does it. The only evidence on usefulness points the other way: the whole knowledge-adaptation stage is worth +0.08 RFS for planning.

### When should slow reasoning run, and does it need to emit a decision?
- **Status**: open

### Advisory VLM or VLM in the action path?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/drivewam.md]]'s frozen Qwen3-VL-8B only emits text guidance and helps at every data scale, but the paper reports no VLM–action agreement rate, and Senna-2's evidence is that unaligned guidance under-delivers. No paper compares the advisory arrangement against fine-tuning the VLM into the action path at matched backbone and data, or distils the VLM into the fast policy after alignment. A third arrangement sits between the two: [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]] keeps ReCogDrive's 2B VLM frozen and silent, and uses its hidden states only to condition a DiT that corrects a separate planner's proposals. See the new question below on where the VLM should enter. A frozen VLM in the action path without decoding: [[sources/drivewla.md]] feeds InternVL3-2B's last-layer states through a trainable adapter into shared attention with a video DiT and the action DiT. It is worth +1.7 PDMS over the video-only model (89.1 → 90.8).

### Where should a VLM enter a strong E2E planner: proposing, fusing, scoring or refining?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: The wiki's VLM planners put the VLM at different points, never on matched backbones. **Generating**: [[sources/recogdrive.md]]'s VLM hidden states condition a DiT that generates whole trajectories (90.8 with RL). **Proposing**: [[sources/lwdrive.md]]'s VLM gives a coarse plan that BEV features refine over six stages (92.0). **Scoring**: [[sources/clear.md]]'s LLM hidden states set the candidate budget and score generated trajectories (93.7). **Refining**: [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]] conditions only a residual refiner over a DrivoR-family planner's proposals (94.8 trainval). **Advising**: [[sources/drivewam.md]] passes text guidance. ChainFlow-VLA gives the only within-paper evidence. Conditioning the refiner on the VLM adds +0.7 over the planner's own scene tokens. A VLM fine-tuned on action QA adds nothing (94.11 against 94.1); one fine-tuned on environment and trajectory QA adds +0.6. The same DiT generating whole trajectories instead of residuals scores 92.89, below the proposals it was given (94.0). Its claim that late conditioning beats early fusion has no same-VLM fusion arm; it rests on cross-paper rows (feature-fusion LatentVLA at 92.4). Nor does it separate what the VLM knows from what its own vision encoder sees. **Fusing, measured within one paper**: [[sources/drivewla.md]]'s frozen VLM shares attention with a single-pass video backbone in every block and adds +1.7 over the same model without it. It also needs the exchange to run both ways (+2.4 over none). Its base is weaker than ChainFlow-VLA's (89.1 against 94.0 before the VLM), so the two increments are not comparable.

## 5. RL, selection and diversity

### Do learned scorers overfit the benchmark's own metric?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Almost every non-BoN NAVSIM-v1 entry above 92.0 PDMS trains a scorer or an RL reward against the benchmark metric. [[sources/mm-future.md]] shows what that looks like on v2: ego progress 92.2 (human agent 87.4) with driving direction, lane keeping and history comfort the lowest in its table, because its scorer learned the v1 components. Whether PDMS-supervised routing and scoring stay reliable in interactive, reactive settings is untested. [[sources/simscale.md]] extends the pattern to training data: its simulated states, its expert trajectories and its reward labels are all filtered or computed with EPDMS, and it is evaluated only on NAVSIM. [[sources/world4scorer.md]] goes one step further on v2: it runs the metric's own extended-comfort check at inference against the previous plan (EC 77.0 → 92.8, above the human agent's 90.1; +1.6 EPDMS, and +1.61 when applied to DrivoR). Its scorer predicts only v1 components, and how its separate v2 combination weights were chosen is not stated. It reaches the world-model VLAs too: DriveVLA-W0's 90.2★ and [[sources/hyworldvla.md]]'s 90.59 both come from a Hydra-MDP-style vocabulary head whose targets combine the expert trajectory with the evaluation metrics. On the same backbones, flow-matching heads without metric targets score 87.2 and 88.76, gaps (+3.0, +1.83) larger than either paper's world-model effect. [[sources/maneuverflow.md]] is another scorer-cohort entry: five PDM-factor heads trained on NAVSIM v1 evaluator labels for the model's own 64 candidates at every training step, and a refiner trained toward the evaluator-best candidate. Its v1 checkpoint scores 90.14 EPDMS on v2 without v2 training, with EC 86.5 and LK 96.9, so the v1-trained scorer does not show MM-Future's v2 rule-term deficit. [[sources/drivor.md]], the base of World4Scorer, trains its scorer on the NAVSIM oracle's sub-scores for every candidate and combines them with weights changed per benchmark; its v2 weights were tuned on a 7-scene validation set, and its demonstration that re-weighting changes behavior uses a differently trained checkpoint (see the new question on re-weighting below). [[sources/clover.md]] is the strongest form yet: pseudo-experts built from the metric cache (including future occupancy) and filtered by the evaluator, and a scorer refit to the evaluator's labels on the generator's own proposals every cycle. It gains +0.74 PDMS on navtest and nothing on navhard or on v2 against its base. Its isolated-scorer diagnostic shows the train–test gap growing with backbone capacity (DINOv2-S 95.0 / 92.4, Wan2.2-5B 97.7 / 92.8). A counter-example from closed loop: [[sources/sparsedrivev2.md]]'s Bench2Drive model trains its factorized scorer by imitation alone, with no metric labels (CARLA has no PDM teacher), and reaches 89.15 DS, the highest non-VLM entry on [[concepts/bench2drive.md]]. On NAVSIM the same design distils the rule-based teacher's sub-scores on the 400 candidates per scene that its imitation-trained filter keeps, labelled during training, and reaches 92.0 PDMS. The two are different models, so the gap between them is not a measurement of what the metric adds. The same pattern in an RL reward on nuScenes: [[sources/worldrft.md]] rewards −1 for each trajectory point whose ego box overlaps an annotated agent box, which is what nuScenes' collision rate counts, and reports collision 0.15% → 0.05% with L2 unchanged (a handful of samples on about 6,000). On NAVSIM the same reward moves DAC and EP (+0.8 each) more than NC (+0.3) or TTC (0.0). The newest top entry, [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]] (94.8 trainval), scores its refined candidates with a head "following iPad" whose labels it does not state, on top of DrivoR's oracle-trained stack. It reports v1 only, and its gain over DrivoR is mostly EP (90.0 → 91.9). Over-optimization is now measured directly. [[sources/toad.md]] maximizes DrivoR's oracle-trained scorer at test time. On 7 validation scenes EPDMS peaks after 5 CEM iterations and falls from 48.5 to 37.2 by 50. In two traced scenes the scorer's reward rises to 1.000 and 0.996 while true EPDMS moves by 0.066 and 0.009. Scorers trained on fixed vocabularies (GTRS, SparseDriveV2) fail outright as search rewards. See the new question below on how hard a scorer can be optimized against.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-19

### How hard can a planner optimize against its own learned scorer before it exploits it?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/toad.md]] is the first paper to optimize a driving scorer directly at test time. A CEM search maximizes DrivoR's oracle-trained sub-score scorer, inside a trust region around the planner's pick. On a 7-scene validation set EPDMS rises from 34.7 to 48.5 at 5 iterations, then falls to 37.2 at 50. In two traced scenes over 100 iterations the reward saturates (0.131 → 1.000, 0.492 → 0.996) while true EPDMS barely moves (+0.023, +0.008 from the first iterate). Removing the anchor term costs 4.1, and a standard-normal start instead of the warm start costs 5.8. Scorers trained only on fixed vocabularies are exploited at once (GTRS's: iPad 34.7 → 23.9). The scorer never predicts NAVSIM-v2's extended comfort, lane keeping or traffic-light terms, which TOAD covers only partly with a closed-form comfort penalty. The same tension appears with training-time optimization: [[sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md]]'s RL against PDMS raises NAVSIM ego progress while closed-loop progress in AlpaSim falls, and [[sources/dreameraD.md]] runs RL against a learned latent reward. No paper reports true score against reward over a full split, or scorers trained to stay calibrated off their proposals (adversarial negatives, ensembles, uncertainty) as a remedy.
- **See**: [[sources/toad.md]], [[concepts/selection-based-planning.md#toad]], [[concepts/inference-time-safety.md#toad]], [[sources/drivor.md]], [[sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md]], [[sources/dreameraD.md]], [[concepts/rl-for-ad.md]]

### Can re-weighting a sub-score scorer at inference change driving behavior without retraining?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/drivor.md]] trains six PDMS sub-score heads and combines them at inference with weights it changes per benchmark (standard PDMS weights for NAVSIM-v1; NC 10, DAC 13, DDC 6, TTC 14, EP 15, Comfort 2 for v2, tuned on a 7-scene set). Its "safety-oriented agent" trades progress for safety on the validation set, but it is the NAVSIM-v2 checkpoint, trained for 10 epochs on navtrain rather than 25 on trainval, and the paper's figure shows different candidate sets for the two agents, which re-weighting alone cannot produce. [[sources/hydra-mdp-pp.md]]'s grid-searched weights (+1.5 PDMS) and [[sources/world4scorer.md]]'s separate v2 weights are the same lever, also tuned against the benchmark. No paper evaluates one checkpoint under several weightings on held-out scenes or in closed loop.

### Does training a generator on its own scorer's choices improve the candidates, or only how well they are ranked?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/clover.md]] trains DrivoR's generator toward evaluator-filtered pseudo-experts (Stage 1), then toward its own scorer's top-$k$ and Pareto picks while refitting the scorer to evaluator labels every cycle (Stage 2). By its own oracle/regret identity on navtest, Stage 1 raises the 64-candidate oracle 99.33 → 99.76 and top-1 93.69 → 94.13 with regret unchanged; Stage 2 drops the oracle to 99.39 and cuts regret 5.63 → 4.95. Of its +0.74 over DrivoR, +0.05 is candidates and +0.69 ranking. The loop is needed (without scorer refits 93.8–94.0; a non-iterative update collapses below 10), and scorers trained in isolation on its fixed pools select 92.4–92.8, about 2 points under the deployed one. [[sources/world4scorer.md]] finds a better scorer worth +0.50 at fixed candidates and its own generator worth nothing on top; [[sources/maneuverflow.md]]'s selector captures less of the oracle as the pool grows. No paper scores one generator's pools with another paper's co-trained scorer, which would separate better candidates from candidates the scorer can rank. A test-time version without training: [[sources/toad.md]] searches beyond the pool with DrivoR's scorer as the reward. On DrivoR's pools, which already have an oracle of 99.33, it gains +0.1 PDMS (on the +SimScale model) and +1.7 navhard EPDMS. On weak generators (iPad) it gains +1.7 PDMS and +15.1 EPDMS, 72% of it from re-ranking alone. Its conclusion that "the set, not the scorer, is the real bottleneck" therefore holds for weak planners and is contradicted at the top of v1 by its own DrivoR result and by CLOVER's decomposition.

- **Updated**: 2026-10-19

### When every candidate is refined but only the expert-closest one has a target, does refinement help selection?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]] refines all $K$ autoregressive proposals with a residual DiT and then scores the refined set. In training, the diffusion loss uses only the proposal closest to the expert, so the other $K-1$ corrections are applied at test time with no residual target behind them. Its refiner is worth +0.1 PDMS conditioned on the planner's own tokens and +0.7 on a frozen VLM's states (single runs). It reports no oracle, so it cannot say whether refinement raises the best available candidate or only moves the ones the scorer prefers. The other refine-and-select designs differ in exactly this respect. [[sources/maneuverflow.md]] trains its refiner toward the evaluator-best candidate of the same maneuver family and re-scores the refined 16 with the other 48 (+1.68 PDMS without future evidence, +0.65 with). [[sources/momworld.md]] refines only the selected plan and does not re-score it. [[sources/clover.md]]'s evaluator-filtered pseudo-experts would give several targets per scene. The per-candidate-future scorers have the same gap: only the expert-matched future is supervised (31 of 32, 63 of 64 not; see "Are per-candidate futures really futures" above). No paper compares one-target and per-candidate-target refinement under one scorer. A refinement with no target at all: [[sources/toad.md]] moves only the planner's pick, by search against a scorer that was trained on all candidates, and keeps the result only if the scorer prefers it. It gains on every planner tested. But the search over-optimizes after about five iterations, so the scorer's accuracy off its training proposals is the binding constraint.

### Is a dense enough fixed vocabulary as good as generated proposals?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/sparsedrivev2.md]] retrains Hydra-MDP with 1,024 → 16,384 anchors (+2.33 EPDMS, about +0.5 per doubling, no saturation before memory runs out; evaluator and backbone unstated). It then factorizes trajectories into 1,024 paths × 256 speed profiles and reaches 92.0 PDMS / 90.1 corrected EPDMS on a ResNet-34, above the generated-proposal rows in its tables (DiffusionDriveV2 91.2 / 87.5, iPad 91.7). But only 400 composed trajectories (200 on v2) reach its metric-trained scorer, chosen per scene by an imitation-trained filter, so its deployed candidate set is scene-dependent like DriveSuprim's (8,192 → 256) and Auto-JEPA's (300 retrieved of 110,335). The oracle evidence points the other way: [[sources/drivesuprim.md]] puts 98.7 PDMS within 256 of 8,192 candidates, [[sources/clover.md]]'s 64 generated proposals have an oracle of 99.33–99.76 and its gain is ranking, and [[sources/idol.md]]'s anchors are flat from 128 to 512 (89.8 / 90.0 / 89.6). Coverage matters for [[sources/ad-e2e-jepa.md]]'s endpoint-matching search (+5.6 EPDMS from 256 to 8,192), which has no learned scorer. No paper compares a dense grid with generated proposals under the same encoder and scorer, or reports the oracle over a factorized grid, which would say whether density buys candidates or easier ranking. In the other direction, fewer generated proposals beat more fixed anchors in one swap: [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]] replaces DiffusionDrive's 20 clustered anchors with 6 autoregressive proposals plus a residual refiner (no VLM) and gains +0.8 PDMS (88.1 → 88.9, the baseline the published row, not a rerun). The anchors and the refiner change together. A different cost of fixed vocabularies: their scorers do not transfer off the grid. In [[sources/toad.md]], GTRS's scorer as a test-time search reward drops iPad from 34.7 to 23.9 navhard EPDMS. SparseDriveV2's leaves iPad at 34.6 and drops Hydra-MDP from 40.9 to 29.6, and as a re-ranker of Hydra-MDP's proposals it gives 9.5. DrivoR's scorer, trained on freely generated proposals, lifts both. An interface mismatch is not ruled out for the worst collapses.

### Does RL buy out-of-distribution robustness?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Within [[sources/geowam.md]]'s navhard table, which contains no candidate-scoring method, three of the four methods above 31 use RL or PDMS-score supervision, but GeoWAM (36.6) tops them without it. Margins are 0.5–2.5 points on single runs, so the wiki cannot say. Whether GRPO-trained NAVSIM methods (FLARE, DriveFine) are competitive on Bench2Drive's interactive scenarios is also unreported. Data without RL buys a good deal of it: [[sources/simscale.md]]'s off-log simulation data, with no RL, adds +5.1 to +8.6 navhard EPDMS across three planner families, more than any margin in GeoWAM's table. Whether RL on the same simulated states would add to that is untested. One closed-loop data point outside NAVSIM: RL in 3DGS cuts collisions on held-out reconstructed clips from 0.229 to 0.089 against the same architecture's imitation-only post-training ([[sources/rad.md]]), but in the training renderer, against non-reactive agents, on a private benchmark whose second instance halves the effect. Later post-training papers add evidence without the control. [[sources/simvta.md]] (RL plus rendered video targets on Epona) scores 38.8 HD-Score on a 345-scenario HUGSIM set, but neither its initialization nor its RL-only arm is run there. [[sources/clover.md]]'s evaluator-supervised generator–scorer loop (supervised on evaluator labels, not policy-gradient RL) is +0.8 PDMS on navtest and +0.0 on navhard over its base DrivoR. [[sources/worldrft.md]]'s collision-reward GRPO reports no out-of-distribution test. Closed-loop data without RL also buys some, in CARLA: [[sources/rog-dagger.md]]'s DAgger post-training of SimLingo (expert labels, no reward) raises Fail2Drive's generalization-split success rate 55.0 → 66.0 and DS 71.7 → 74.7, while the base split's DS falls 2.4. Behaviour scenarios gain +30.8 harmonic mean and robustness scenarios lose 6.1. TF++ (LiDAR, no post-training) has the highest DS on both splits. Single runs, and the SimLingo baseline row's origin is not stated.

### Does RL against log-replayed agents' collisions carry over to agents that react?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Two RL rewards in the wiki count collisions with agents replayed from the log, which cannot respond to the ego. [[sources/worldrft.md]] rewards −1 per trajectory point overlapping an annotated box and cuts nuScenes collision 0.15% → 0.05% (a handful of samples on about 6,000; the metric counts the same overlaps), while on NAVSIM NC moves +0.3 and TTC not at all. [[sources/rad.md]] penalizes collisions with log-replayed agents in 3DGS and reports 0.229 → 0.089 against its own imitation-only post-training on a private benchmark of the same kind; on [[sources/senna2.md]]'s benchmark from the same institutions, with agents described as reactive, RAD's collision ratio is 0.281 and its advantage over VADv2 halves ([[concepts/evaluation-variance.md]]). Neither paper evaluates the same checkpoint before and after RL against reactive agents, which would separate collision avoidance from fitting the replayed log.

### Does reinforcement learning from camera input beat imitation at matched data and backbone?
- **Status**: open

### How should diversity be rewarded and measured when the output is text plus a trajectory?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Best-of-6 on NAVSIM-v1 matches the human trajectory's score (Curious-VLA, 94.8 PDMS). ### Is a reward needed for closed-loop training, or are future-anchored action labels enough?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]] trains a deployed policy on its own rollouts with no reward: a plan head conditioned on the recorded future labels every state the policy reaches. Lane keeping and lane changes emerge (24/24, 19–20/20 against 5/24, 8/20 off-policy). The policy can at best match its teacher, and the teacher is never evaluated on its own. Reward-based closed-loop training exists in other simulators ([[sources/senna2.md]]: penalties in 3DGS; [[sources/dreameraD.md]]: a learned latent reward), but no paper compares labels against reward in one simulator. [[sources/simscale.md]] compares expert-trajectory labels with per-trajectory benchmark sub-scores (a reward signal, used by regression, not policy gradient) for one scorer in one 3DGS simulator: sub-scores win by +0.3 / +0.8 navhard EPDMS, within single-run noise, and only when expert imitation is kept on the real data (dropping it costs −0.7 / −3.1). The opposite end of the comparison is now ingested: [[sources/think2drive.md]] uses a hand-shaped reward and no labels in a reactive simulator (CARLA v2), and its policy then became the label source for Bench2Drive. Neither paper trains the other's way. In one 3DGS simulator, [[sources/rad.md]] compares off-policy imitation, RL and both (collision ratio 0.229 / 0.143 / 0.089 over 337 clips), and finds PPO without hand-designed directional auxiliary losses no better than imitation (0.249). Its labels are the logged expert's, not on-policy ones, so the openpilot comparison is still missing. **Labels without a reward, in a reactive simulator, now have a Bench2Drive result.** [[sources/rog-dagger.md]] fine-tunes SimLingo on a privileged expert's labels for its own closed-loop CARLA states (one DAgger round, about 51K samples): +3.75 DS / +7.15 SR over its base (90.34 / 73.51), and Longest6 v2 22 → 44 DS. TakeVLA (not ingested) post-trains the same base model with takeover data plus RL in reconstructed scenarios and lands level (89.72 / 73.73). The bases differ by 1.5 DS and both are single runs, so it is still not a matched labels-vs-reward comparison ([[concepts/rl-for-ad.md#rog-dagger]]).

### Do policies trained in rendered simulators learn the renderer's artifacts?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]] states that reprojection artifacts correlate with the ego's offset from the log, which is what the corrective label encodes, and that the policy exploits them ("cheating or shortcut learning"). Its remedy is a roughly 700-bit Gaussian-noise bottleneck on the feature extractor. Neither the exploitation nor the remedy is measured, and whether the world-model simulator has the same problem is not discussed. No other training paper in the wiki (3DGS-based or world-model-based) reports a test for it. An evaluation-side version now exists. [[sources/simscale.md]] trains on 3DGS renders of off-log poses and gains most on navhard's 3DGS-rendered Stage 2 (+5.6 to +7.6 per-stage EPDM, against +0.8 to +5.3 in Stage 1 and +0.8 to +2.9 on real-image navtest). Its training engine (MTGS) and the navhard benchmark paper share two author names with it; whether Stage 2 uses the same renderer is not stated. No probe and no zero-perturbation rendering control is reported. [[sources/rad.md]] compares a policy trained on 3DGS renders against imitation baselines that never saw one, evaluated in the same renderer, and ends every training episode at a collision or deviation because the renders degrade afterwards. Neither confound is tested. [[sources/simvta.md]] trains a world-action model's encoder on 3DGS frames (as teacher-forced history and as video targets) and selects for supervision the renders its model predicts worst, which it concedes may be the worst renders. It reports renderer fidelity only along logged trajectories and runs no artifact test. Its HUGSIM evaluation is itself 3DGS, on another group's reconstructions. The same renderer, World Engine, is now also a closed-loop benchmark ([[concepts/worldengine-benchmark.md]], used by [[sources/maneuverflow.md]]), so a policy trained on its renders and tested on its benchmark would face this confound directly; no paper has done both yet.

### Which RL recipe suits dense, safety-gated driving rewards?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Whether Dr. GRPO remains preferable when rewards are dense, continuous and safety-gated rather than binary is untested. It is also unknown at what data scale reasoning-free Dr. GRPO training (NoRD) catches up with CoT-supervised training on 212K+ samples (AutoVLA), and whether a single sequence-level objective can serve both expert types in a mixture-of-experts planner. One controlled data point outside NAVSIM: in CARLA, a model-based actor-critic trained in latent imagination beats PPO with identical engineering, 83.8 vs 57.5 DS on single-scenario routes and 98.6% vs 1% route completion on long routes ([[sources/think2drive.md]]). Its account of PPO's failure is that a mid-training policy reset, needed to escape conflicting scenario optima, is cheap only when a world model supplies dense rewards. The ablation behind it has no numbers in the clipping. From cameras, the same recipe works only when bootstrapped from a privileged system: [[sources/raw2drive.md]]'s RL fine-tuning adds +25 Dev10 DS over transplanting the privileged policy (58.4 → 83.5), and the system scores 0 without its latent alignment. In 3DGS with camera input, [[sources/rad.md]]'s PPO needs three additions to help: per-axis advantages and clipping over a factorized lateral / longitudinal action, rule-directed auxiliary losses weighted by the advantage (without them 0.249 CR against 0.229 for imitation), and imitation steps interleaved 4:1 (without them the policy drifts, 0.345 m from the expert). On a world-action model with the simulator reward, [[sources/simvta.md]] uses Flow-GRPO's SDE without an importance ratio or KL, a per-coordinate clipped log-density score, a 0.6 denoising discount and a BC anchor to the frozen initial head's ODE chain: +2.9 PDMS on Epona (86.2 → 89.1, NC −0.7, three seeds). [[sources/dreameraD.md]]'s vocabulary sampling with a learned latent reward got +2.5 from the same initialization; not a controlled comparison. The sparse end, on a regression head: [[sources/worldrft.md]] wraps a deterministic trajectory regressor in a Gaussian with a learned variance, samples $G=10$, and rewards −1 per point that collides with a logged agent's box, nothing else. Advantages are group-normalized per point and summed to the horizon; regularizers are a Gaussian NLL of the reference trajectory (called KL), an L2 to the reference mean and an entropy bonus. +0.8 PDMS and nuScenes collision 0.15 → 0.05, single runs. With a binary reward most groups have zero variance, and their handling is not stated; RL on 10% of the data recovers little (0.15 → 0.12). [[sources/dreameraD.md]]'s re-implementation of this sampler inside its own world model scores 86.6 EPDMS against 87.7 for its vocabulary sampler. Factorizing over the evaluator's sub-scores: [[sources/drivewla.md]] standardizes NC, DAC, EP, TTC and comfort separately within each Flow-GRPO group. It weights them 2 / 2 / 5 / 5 / 2, additively, so NC and DAC are no longer gates, and shrinks low-variance groups by $s^2/(s^2+\tau^2)$. That is worth +0.2 PDMS over scalar-PDMS Flow-GRPO (92.1 → 92.3), whose own gain is +1.3. RL is run only on training scenes the imitation policy scores below 90, on the action LoRA (single runs).

### Does simulated off-log data keep paying as it grows, and which planners can use it?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/simscale.md]] adds up to 237K simulated scenes to about 100K real ones (2.3:1). Under PDM-Closed labels a regression planner (LTF) flattens near 1:1 while DiffusionDrive and a GTRS-Dense scorer keep rising; under retrieval labels that steer back to the log, every planner peaks early. The gain from simulation does not shrink as real data grows from 10K to 100K (+5.6 to +7.5 EPDMS for ResNet-34, read from the figure). Whether an on-policy sampler in the same renderer would scale differently is unknown. Per scene, more rendered targets do not help in [[sources/simvta.md]]: doubling the rendering budget from four to eight candidates gives 91.2 against 91.3 PDMS (seed means 91.17 vs 91.33), and supervising all rendered targets instead of the higher-error half gives 91.0. [[sources/drivor.md]] (camera-only ViT-S, 64 learned queries) keeps rising at 65k and 134k scenes: navhard 48.3 → 52.3 → 54.6, v1 93.7 → 94.0 → 94.6, passing GTRS-Dense trained on 185k (53.2). Three points per curve, single runs.

### Should a pseudo-expert be conservative or exploratory?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: In one simulator, [[sources/simscale.md]]'s conservative teacher (retrieving the human trajectory that returns to the log) helps at small data sizes and then stops helping; the exploratory one (PDM-Closed on ground-truth state) keeps paying but passes on its poor comfort. Averaging scorers trained on both, plus a reward-only one, adds +0.8 / +2.9 navhard EPDMS. openpilot's teacher ([[sources/learning-to-drive-from-a-world-model.md]]) is a third kind, a learned plan head that sees the future, and has never been compared with either. A third kind, exploratory and evaluator-filtered: [[sources/clover.md]] builds 261 rule-based candidates per scene from the metric cache (lateral shifts, speed profiles, stop-go, boundary cases), keeps those the evaluator scores ≥ 0.8, and asks the generator to cover up to 8 of them alongside the logged trajectory. That is worth +0.4 PDMS alone and raises the 64-candidate oracle 99.33 → 99.76, with a heavier low-score tail that its later self-distillation removes. Single runs, NAVSIM only. On-policy and in CARLA, [[sources/rog-dagger.md]] widens a conservative expert. PDM-Lite brakes along its fixed route; RoG-Expert also tries lateral shifts (0.2–3 m) and, in junctions, 25% and 50% of the current speed, and keeps the first plan a kinematic rollout finds collision-free, preferring speed, then a small shift. Removing the expansion costs −1.63 DS / −4.57 SR on Bench2Drive (single run), the largest SR drop among its components. The paper credits the wider expert for its student steering to the free side of a construction site where the base model steers toward oncoming traffic (Fail2Drive's Construction Pedestrian scenario, one qualitative example).

### What makes a privileged expert a good on-policy teacher: its driving score, or the feasibility, timing and observability of its labels?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: [[sources/rog-dagger.md]] runs DAgger in CARLA with two privileged experts for one camera student (SimLingo-Qwen3, 86.59 DS / 66.36 SR). PDM-Lite (97.02 / 92.27 on its own) yields 87.85 / 66.66. RoG-Expert (93.41 / 81.81), which is PDM-Lite with rollout-checked replanning, a takeover trigger near the last solvable state and blinding to actors the student cannot see, yields 90.34 / 73.51. Its single-run ablations price each property: solution-space expansion −1.63 DS / −4.57 SR, solvability-aware takeover −1.69 / −3.65, field-of-view alignment −1.38 / −3.65, and dropping the pre-takeover labels −2.29 / −5.47. ## 6. Efficiency

### What does each method actually cost at inference?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Recorded latencies span 22 ms to 9.4 s on different hardware, and only the Wan2.2-5B family is close to comparable (Metis and SUV both use an RTX 4090). Missing figures matter most where the cost is likely highest: GRAVA's grounded reasoning (8B, 4,096-token limit) reports none, SUV reports only its with-access cost, and [[sources/physwam.md]] pays 9.4 GPU-seconds per plan with no action-only path tested. Per-candidate futures are cheap once the state is a few hundred tokens (about 3.2 ms per hypothesis in [[sources/mm-future.md]]). [[sources/raw2drive.md]] separates its costs: 600 ms for the BEVFormer camera encoder, 2 ms each for world model and policy (hardware unstated). [[sources/foredrive.md]]: 58.2 ms on an H20 for 90.0 EPDMS, of which latent foresight is 26 ms; the fastest entry at or above 90 EPDMS. [[sources/world4scorer.md]]: 106.5 ms fp32 on an A100 (23.0 ms fp16) for 94.0 PDMS with 35.5M parameters, plus 125 ms of CPU re-ranking in its v2 configuration; the scorer itself takes 1.4 ms. [[sources/hyworldvla.md]] (Emu3-8B VLA plus action expert) reports no latency; its base DriveVLA-W0 reports 74 ms for the same head. [[sources/idol.md]]: about 57 ms (17.65 FPS) on an RTX 3090 for 256 per-anchor latent BEV rollouts on 64 tokens, two passes, 69.4M parameters. [[sources/maneuverflow.md]] reports "Proposal FPS" of 53.86 at 64 candidates, but only for perception through its one-step generator; the world model, evidence probe, two scoring passes and refiner are untimed and the timing hardware is unstated. [[sources/drivor.md]]: 110 ms on an A100 (107 ms of it the ViT-S over four 672×1148 cameras, 3 ms for the decoders), with a same-setup table timing GTRS-Dense V2-99 at 96 ms, ZTRS 193 ms, GTRS-Aug 243 ms, GTRS-Dense ViT-L 400 ms and RAP-DINO 690 ms. [[sources/dvgt-2.md]]: about 0.26–0.27 s and about 70 GB per 8-view frame, constant over 1,200+ frames, 1.8B parameters, hardware not named; geometry and planning together, no planning-only timing. At the first frame it is already about twice as fast as its predecessor DVGT, so not all of the speed-up is the feature cache. [[sources/clover.md]]: about 110 ms per scene on an A100 at 64 candidates, DrivoR's own figure for the same graph; its v2 configuration also runs a separate anchor planner, and whether that is included is not stated. Training takes 4–5 days on four A100s plus a 64-core CPU for evaluator calls. [[sources/sparsedrivev2.md]] names efficient scoring of a 262,144-combination vocabulary as a contribution and reports no latency or memory for itself. Its only cost figure is training memory for the Hydra-MDP baseline: 9.5 GB at 1,024 anchors to 38.9 GB at 16,384, with 32,768 out of memory on a 48 GB L20. [[sources/worldrft.md]] runs a frozen VGGT on every camera at every frame beside a ResNet-34 (NAVSIM) or ResNet-50 (nuScenes) and reports no latency; the VGGT is worth +1.3 PDMS in its ablation. [[sources/law.md]]'s arXiv v1 reports 19.5 FPS (about 51 ms; RTX 3090 per [[sources/momworld.md]]'s table) for its nuScenes perception-free model, whose world model runs only in training; v2 drops the figure. [[sources/chainflow-vla.md]], the highest v1 entry (94.8), reports none for DrivoR's backbone plus a $K$-way autoregressive decoder, a 2B InternVL forward pass and $K$ residual DiT samples ($K$ unstated). It keeps 4 denoising steps over the higher-scoring 12 "to balance performance and inference efficiency" without a timing. [[sources/lvdrive.md]]: 2.03 s per frame on an H20 at batch 1 for a Vicuna-7B VLA that prefills about 1,550 placeholder tokens for six future frames, against 0.93 s for its planning-only base. A matched autoregressive variant would take 36.62 s. Its text calls this a speed-up over the base. [[sources/toad.md]]: the search adds 1.9 ms (5 CEM iterations) to 20.4 ms (100) on an A100, so DrivoR + TOAD is 104.5 ms. Any other planner also runs DrivoR's ViT-S encoder for the scorer (~100 ms): iPad 9.2 → 118.8 ms, Hydra-MDP 181.1 → 300.9 ms. [[sources/raf-vla.md]]: 153 ms on an H200 at batch 1 for a Qwen2.5-VL-7B Mixture-of-Transformers VLA with a front-camera sequence; its eight world queries add 1 ms, and their alignment loss adds 3.8% to SFT time (10.56 → 10.96 h on 4 × H200). [[sources/drivewla.md]]: 970 ms at 10 Euler steps (540 ms at one step, where PDMS collapses to 49.2), with no hardware named, for a 2B video DiT over four 768×1344 frames, a frozen InternVL3-2B and a 28-block action DiT.

### Do PDMS and EPDMS agree with human judgment of which trajectory is better?

### Do different planners fail on the same scenes?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Per-scene overlap has been measured only within one model: [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]]'s exits share 0.69–0.82 (Jaccard) of their scenes scoring ≥ 90 after RL ([[concepts/foundation-backbones-for-ad.md]]). Paired per-scene variance exists for two checkpoints of one model family (a 95% half-width of about 0.21 EPDMS, [[sources/drivereferee.md]]) and is unmeasured across methods ([[concepts/evaluation-variance.md]]). Most of DrivoR's own shortfall is recoverable within its pool: 93.69 PDMS for its scorer against 99.33 for the oracle ([[concepts/best-of-n.md]]). If failures are shared across methods, the remaining headroom is a set small enough to inspect by hand, including for label and metric errors (see the human-judgment question above). Released checkpoints suffice ([[sources/drivor.md]], [[sources/sparsedrivev2.md]]).

### How much of the NAVSIM ordering is the sensor set (cameras vs. cameras + LiDAR)?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: NAVSIM tables mix one-front-camera VLAs with camera + LiDAR planners such as [[sources/diffusiondrive.md]]. The one matched swap is [[sources/foredrive.md]]'s: replacing DiffusionDrive's camera + LiDAR BEV with one front camera and a DINOv3 ViT-S costs 1.7 PDMS (88.1 → 86.4), with the encoder changed at the same time ([[concepts/diffusion-planner.md]]). For perception the gap is larger: camera-only BEV detection reaches 25.0 mAP against 58.9 with LiDAR initialization in [[sources/percept-wam.md]] ([[concepts/perception-for-planning.md]]). Even the labels are unstable: two papers print the same navhard row as LTF (camera-only) and as TransFuser (camera + LiDAR) ([[concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md]]). The camera question in section 3 ("How many visual tokens does a planner need, and from which cameras?") does not cover LiDAR.
- **See**: [[concepts/perception-for-planning.md]], [[concepts/diffusion-planner.md]], [[concepts/navhard-ood-evaluation.md]], [[sources/foredrive.md]], [[sources/percept-wam.md]]

### Does a planner degrade gracefully when a camera fails or its calibration shifts?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Weather is the only robustness axis the wiki records: [[sources/hyworldvla.md]]'s 655 rain/fog scenes (see "Does world-model supervision buy robustness to sensor degradation?" in section 2) and [[sources/momworld.md]]'s nuScenes-C results, which use snow, rain and fog out of 27 corruption types at five severities ([[concepts/nuscenes-waymo-evals.md#six-second]]). No page drops a camera, perturbs extrinsics or delays a frame. [[sources/drivor.md]] is a ready first case: its back camera's 16 registers collapse to about one distinct token, yet its scorer draws on side and rear cameras for sharp turns (see "How many visual tokens does a planner need, and from which cameras?" in section 3).

### Does inference latency change closed-loop driving when the simulator does not wait?
- **Status**: open
- **Known so far**: Recorded latencies span 22 ms to 9.4 s ([[concepts/inference-latency.md]]). In the one case the wiki records, the closed-loop simulator waits for the planner: [[sources/physwam.md]]'s HUGSIM result serves a 0.5 s replanning interval with a 9.4 GPU-second computation. Means also understate the budget: [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]]'s router averages 170 ms at η = 90 but 284 ms at η = 95, above its 190 ms fixed baseline ([[concepts/adaptive-routing.md]]), and almost no paper reports peak latency. No page executes each plan only after its measured latency. Related: "What does each method actually cost at inference?" in section 6.
- **See**: [[concepts/inference-latency.md]], [[concepts/adaptive-routing.md]], [[sources/physwam.md]], [[sources/adaptive-wam.md]]
- **Updated**: 2026-10-25

### Does a NAVSIM planner generalize to a city held out of training?

## Answered

Questions closed by an ingested paper, each with the paper and its result.

- **Is the comfort deficit inherent to sampled planners?** No. [[sources/physwam.md]] samples video, depth and motion from noise and scores 96.3 closed-loop comfort and 90.5 navtest EC. What it does differently from WA-JEPA, which scores far lower on closed-loop comfort, is not isolated. See [[concepts/hugsim-benchmark.md]]. (2026-09-30)
- **Does performance on curated rare-event clips predict closed-loop behaviour?** No. The AlpaSim reproduction in [[sources/qwen-drive-1.0.md]] inverts the ordering. The remaining open part is tracked under "Do NAVSIM rankings survive a reactive closed loop?". See [[concepts/physicalai-av-benchmark.md]]. (2026-09-30)
